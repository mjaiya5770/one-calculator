How creators actually get paid

If you make videos, podcasts, streams or newsletters for income, your money arrives through four doors — platform revenue, sponsorships, direct sales and client work — and every door has a different toll. This hub exists so you can see each toll before you walk through.

RPM is not CPM

Advertisers pay CPM — a price per 1,000 ad impressions. You receive RPM — revenue per 1,000 views, after the platform's share and after counting every view that carried no ad at all. RPM is always the smaller number, and it is the only one that spends. On YouTube long-form, creators keep 55% of ad revenue; on TikTok, view rewards come from a program pool whose per-view rate floats with region and watch quality. Any calculator that quotes you a CPM as if it were your income is inflating your expectations by roughly half.

The fee stack takes a second cut

Platform revenue is only the first layer. When money moves to you — a brand paying an invoice by card, a fan buying a product, a client paying for freelance work — a processor takes roughly 3% plus a fixed fee per transaction. On small payments the fixed fee dominates: 30¢ on a $3 sale is 10% before the percentage applies. The Payment Fee Calculator prices your exact stack, including the gross-up: the charge that leaves you with the amount you actually intended to receive.

Sponsorships are priced on trust, not views alone

For most channels under a million subscribers, one sponsorship pays more than a month of ad revenue — because a brand is buying your audience's trust in a niche, not renting impressions. Rates scale with average views, then bend with engagement (an audience that acts is worth more than one that scrolls), niche buying power, and the scope: a dedicated video costs more than a 60-second integration, and usage rights — the brand running your face as their ad — are a separate, time-limited charge. The Sponsorship Rate Calculator shows every multiplier so you can defend your number line by line.

And now there is an AI line item

Scripts, captions, thumbnails, research — AI tools have become a real production cost for working creators, billed per token in ways that are genuinely hard to eyeball. The LLM API Cost Estimator prices a workload across the major models at their published rates, so the "cheap" model choice is made with numbers instead of vibes. The honest summary of all four sections: know your RPM, mind the fixed fees, price sponsorships on engagement, and never let a tool subscription creep past the revenue it supports.

Creator ROI questions

Which of these tools should a brand-new creator start with?

The earnings calculators for your platform, to set honest expectations, then the Break-Even Calculator to learn what the channel must earn to cover its costs. Sponsorship pricing matters later than most beginners think — and earlier than most growing creators realize.

Are the earnings figures on this hub guaranteed?

No — and be suspicious of any site that guarantees them. Platforms publish no rate cards for creators. Every range here is labeled an editorial planning estimate, the method is shown on each tool, and your own platform analytics replace every estimate the moment you have them.

Why is there no "viral calculator" or follower-count pricing?

Because followers do not get paid — views, engagement and conversions do. Tools that price sponsorships off follower counts flatter sellers and mislead buyers. Everything here starts from views and revenue, the numbers money actually follows.
