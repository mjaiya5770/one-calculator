Drywall Calculator

Drywall is bought by the sheet and hung by the wall — the conversion is where projects under-buy. Enter the room, choose whether the ceiling is in, and get sheets for both panel sizes, a screw estimate, and the material cost.

Openings (doors, windows) are not subtracted — pros order the sheet count whole and cut around them; the offcuts cover mistakes and closets. Screw estimate: about 32 per sheet into studs and joists at standard spacing.

How it works

Wall area = perimeter × ceiling height — (length + width) × 2 × height. The ceiling, if included, adds length × width. Sheets = total area ÷ sheet size (32 sq ft for 4×8, 48 for 4×12), rounded up, because partial sheets still ship as sheets. Screws are estimated at 32 per sheet, the field standard for walls and ceilings at 16-inch spacing.

Estimates for materials planning. Does not subtract openings or add corner bead, tape, mud or waste for breakage — order one or two spare sheets on any real job; returning a sheet is cheaper than stopping a crew.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Wall area = 2 × (length + width) × ceiling height; add length × width when the ceiling is included. Sheets = total area ÷ sheet area (32 sq ft for 4×8, 48 sq ft for 4×12), rounded up. Screws ≈ 32 per sheet at standard spacing.

Worked example: A 12 × 10 ft room with 8 ft ceilings: walls are 352 sq ft and the ceiling 120, totalling 472 sq ft — 15 sheets of 4×8 or 10 sheets of 4×12, about 480 screws, and roughly $225 of panels at $15 a sheet.

When to use this

  • You are finishing a basement bedroom and need a sheet count the store can load in one trip, not a guess from the parking lot.
  • A contractor's material list looks padded and you want to check the sheets and screws against the room you can measure yourself.
  • You are deciding whether a taller ceiling pushes you into a second delivery once the ceiling panels are counted too.
  • You are pricing a garage conversion and need the panel cost before tape, mud and finishing enter the conversation.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Measure the room length and width in feet, wall to wall.
  2. Enter the ceiling height — 9 ft ceilings add a lot more wall area than people expect over 8 ft.
  3. Say whether the ceiling is being boarded as well as the walls.
  4. Pick the sheet size you can actually get into the room, and set the price per sheet from your store.
  5. Read the sheet count for both sizes, the screw estimate and the panel cost together before ordering.

A 16 by 13 ft primary bedroom with 9 ft ceilings, ceiling included: the walls are 522 sq ft and the ceiling adds 208, for 730 sq ft to cover. Frequently asked questions

How many sheets for a 12 × 10 room with 8 ft ceilings?

Walls are 44 ft of perimeter × 8 = 352 sq ft; the ceiling adds 120, for 472 sq ft total. That is 15 sheets of 4×8 or 10 sheets of 4×12, roughly 480 screws, and about $225 in panels at $15 a sheet.

4×8 or 4×12 sheets — which should I buy?

4×12s mean fewer seams to tape and mud, but they are heavier, harder to move through halls and stairs, and near-impossible to hang solo on a ceiling. Most DIY rooms are 4×8 territory; pros with a lift reach for 12s.

How many screws does a sheet take?

About 32: screws every 12 inches in the field and 8 inches on edges at standard framing spacing. A 15-sheet room lands near 480 screws — buy the 5 lb box, not the 1 lb box.

Should I subtract doors and windows?

For sheet count, no — the sheet covering a doorway gets cut, and the offcut is rarely reusable elsewhere. The subtraction method saves at most a sheet and regularly leaves you one short.

What else do I need besides sheets and screws?

Joint tape, joint compound, corner bead, primer and paint are all separate from this count — the calculator prices panels only. The page's own advice stands on the sheets themselves too: order one or two spares on a real job, because returning an unopened sheet is cheaper than stopping work for one broken corner.

What if my room is not a simple rectangle?

Split it into rectangular sections — a main box plus the bump-out, closet bay or hallway stub — and run the calculator once per section. Add the sheet counts, not the room dimensions: combining an L-shape into one big rectangle boards over floor space that is not there and inflates the order.
