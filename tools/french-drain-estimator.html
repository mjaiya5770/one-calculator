French Drain Estimator

A French drain is a gravel trench with a pipe hiding in it — and the gravel is always more than the trench "looks like", minus a pipe's worth everyone forgets to subtract. Enter the run and trench size: get gravel yards and tons, pipe and fabric.

Sized for the standard 4-in perforated pipe. Slope matters more than size: the trench needs at least 1% fall (about ⅛ in per foot) toward daylight or a dry well, or you have built a very long, gravel-filled puddle.

Tip: dimensions accept tape-measure entries — try 6' 8 5/16" or 6.5.

How it works

Gravel fills the trench minus the pipe: trench volume (length × width × depth) minus the 4-in pipe's cylinder volume — on a 50-ft run the pipe displaces about 4.4 cu ft, small but real. Washed drainage stone converts at 1.40 tons per cubic yard — round, washed rock, not crushed base (crushed fines clog drains; that is why drainage calls for washed stone). Pipe is bought to the trench length; add your outlet run to daylight when you order. Fabric lines the trench before stone: order the run length in a roll wide enough to wrap the trench (about 5 ft of wrap width for a standard trench).

Estimates only. Check where your water may legally daylight — many municipalities prohibit discharging onto neighbours or into sanitary sewers. Call before you dig (811 in the US) — trenches find utilities with depressing reliability.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Gravel volume = trench volume (length × width × depth) − pipe volume (π × (2 in radius in ft)² × length for standard 4-in pipe). Washed drainage stone converts at 1.40 tons per cu yd. Pipe and fabric order to the trench length.

Worked example: A 50-ft trench, 12 in wide × 18 in deep: 2.62 cu yd (3.66 tons) of washed stone, 50 ft of pipe, 50 ft of fabric.

When to use this

  • A yard corner stays swampy and you are trenching a drain to daylight.
  • You are ordering washed stone and need pipe and fabric on the same list.
  • A drainage quote lists gravel tonnage you want to check.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Measure the trench run in feet along its actual path.
  2. Pick width and depth for the water problem — 12 × 18 in standard.
  3. Note gravel yards AND tons; yards order the truck, tons verify it.
  4. Add your outlet run beyond the trench when ordering pipe.

Another worked example

A short side-yard drain: 30 ft at 12 in wide and 12 in deep. Trench volume 30 cu ft; the 4-in pipe displaces 2.62 cu ft; gravel = 27.38 cu ft = 1.01 cu yd, about 1.42 tons — a single bulk scoop at most yards, plus 30 ft each of pipe and fabric.

Frequently asked questions

How much gravel for a 50-ft French drain?

A standard 12-in wide, 18-in deep trench: 2.62 cu yd of washed stone — 3.66 tons — after the 4-in pipe's own volume comes out. Plus 50 ft of perforated pipe and a 50-ft run of filter fabric.

How deep should a French drain be?

Twelve to 24 inches covers most yard work: deep enough to intercept water moving through topsoil, shallow enough to daylight at a slope. A 30-ft run at 12-in depth takes about 1.01 cu yd (1.42 tons). Foundation drains sit at footing depth — a different, bigger job.

Washed stone or crushed gravel?

Washed, round-to-subangular drainage stone (often sold as #57 or drain rock). Crushed stone carries fines that migrate into the pipe slots and silt the system shut. The fabric wrap slows that further — it is a filter, not a liner.

Does the pipe go holes-up or holes-down?

Perforated pipe in a French drain goes holes DOWN: water rises into the pipe from below as the trench fills, then flows along it. (Holes-up is for distribution systems like septic fields — different job, opposite logic.)

How much fall does the trench need?

One percent minimum — about 1 inch per 8 feet. More is better; dead level is a storage trench that eventually silts. Laser levels earn their keep on long runs.

Can a French drain handle roof water?

It can, but solid (non-perforated) pipe is the right carrier for downspout water to the drain field — mixing roof volume into a perforated yard drain can overload it and surface water exactly where you buried the pipe.

How long does a French drain last?

A fabric-wrapped drain in washed stone commonly serves 20–40 years; unwrapped drains in fine soils can silt in under a decade. The fabric and stone choices in this estimator are the longevity choices.
