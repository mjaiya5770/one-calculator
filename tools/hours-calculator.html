Hours Calculator

How long is 9:00 to 17:30, minus lunch — and what is it worth? Enter start and end (overnight shifts wrap past midnight on their own), subtract a break, and get the span in hours:minutes, in decimal hours for timesheets, and in gross pay if you add a rate.

An end time earlier than the start is treated as next-day — overnight shifts need no special handling. Timesheets want decimal hours: 8 h 30 m is 8.50, not 8.30.

How it works

Both times become minutes since midnight; the end gains 1,440 minutes if it is earlier than the start (the overnight wrap). Subtract the break and you have the shift. The decimal form — total minutes ÷ 60 — is the one payroll systems use, which is why 30 minutes shows as .50 and 45 minutes as .75.

A calculator, not a payroll service: rounding rules, overtime premiums and break laws vary by employer and jurisdiction — the Overtime Calculator models the premium side.

Method & worked example

Method: Times convert to minutes since midnight; an end earlier than the start gains 1,440 minutes (the overnight wrap). Paid minutes = span − break, and decimal hours = paid minutes ÷ 60 — the form payroll uses, where 30 minutes is .50, not .30.

Worked example: 9:00 to 17:30 spans 8 h 30 m; minus a 30-minute break, the paid time is exactly 8 h — 8.00 decimal hours, $160.00 gross at $20/hr. An overnight shift of 22:00 to 06:00 is 8 hours with no special handling.

When to use this

  • You are filling in a weekly timesheet and the shifts were not tidy eight-hour blocks.
  • You work nights and need to prove to yourself — or to payroll — what a run of overnight shifts actually totals.
  • You are comparing a part-time schedule against your current one and want paid hours, not clock hours.
  • An invoice for hourly contract work needs the billable time from a start and end you wrote down.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Enter the start time and the end time exactly as the clock showed them.
  2. Enter the unpaid break in minutes — total it first if the shift had more than one.
  3. Add your hourly rate if you want the shift's gross pay as well as its length.
  4. Read the paid time in hours and minutes, then take the decimal hours figure for the timesheet.
  5. For a run of shifts, repeat per shift and add the decimal figures.

Another worked example

An overnight shift from 23:15 to 07:45 spans 8 hours 30 minutes. Take off a 45-minute unpaid break and the paid time is 7 hours 45 minutes — 7.75 decimal hours — which grosses $143.38 at $18.50 an hour. The wrap past midnight needs no special handling: the end being earlier than the start is what tells the calculator the shift crossed into the next day.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours is 9:00 to 17:30 with a 30-minute break?

The span is 8 hours 30 minutes; minus the break, 8 hours exactly — 8.00 decimal hours. At $20 an hour that shift grosses $160.00.

How do overnight shifts work here?

Enter them plainly: 22:00 to 06:00 is 8 hours, because an end earlier than the start is read as the next day. No date fields, no checkboxes.

Why does my timesheet say 8.50 for 8 hours 30 minutes?

Payroll runs on decimals: minutes ÷ 60. Thirty minutes is half an hour (.50), fifteen is .25, forty-five is .75. Writing 8.30 on a timesheet underpays you by 12 minutes — a classic quiet loss.

Is the break subtracted before pay is computed?

Yes — gross pay here is paid hours only, so an unpaid break never inflates the total. If your break is paid, set it to 0.

How do I total a whole week of shifts?

Run each shift on its own and add the decimal hours figures — decimals are made for adding, which is exactly why payroll uses them. Five shifts that each come out at 7.75 is 38.75 hours for the week, a sum you can do on paper; adding 7 hours 45 minutes five times in your head is where errors move in.

My shift had two breaks — which one do I enter?

Add them together and enter the total minutes in the single break field. A 15-minute break and a 30-minute meal break are 45 minutes here. If one of the breaks was paid, leave that one out — only unpaid time comes off, and paid break minutes belong in the paid total.
