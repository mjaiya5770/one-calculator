How it works

Words are counted as groups of non-whitespace characters. Sentences split on . ! ? punctuation, paragraphs split on blank lines, and reading time assumes an average adult pace of 200 words per minute. All counting happens in your browser — your text never leaves the page.

Method & worked example

Method: Words are counted as runs of non-whitespace characters — the rule most word processors use. Characters are counted with and without spaces, sentences are split on ., ! and ?, paragraphs on blank lines, and reading time is estimated at 200 words per minute.

Worked example: “The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog.” counts as 9 words, 44 characters (36 without spaces) and 1 sentence — about 3 seconds of reading time.

When to use this

  • An essay, application or assignment has a hard word limit and you need the live count while you cut or pad.
  • A post, listing or bio is limited by characters rather than words and you need to see both numbers at once.
  • You are timing a speech, a presentation script or a video voiceover and want the reading time before you rehearse it.
  • An editor, teacher or client quoted a length and you want to check the draft against it before sending.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Paste or type your text into the box — every statistic updates live as you type, with nothing to submit.
  2. Read the Words figure for word limits, and Characters for character limits; Characters (no spaces) is the figure some forms and print specs ask for instead.
  3. Check Sentences and Paragraphs when a structure rule applies, such as a paragraph count for a school assignment.
  4. Use Reading time to judge spoken or read-aloud length — it is worked out at 200 words per minute.
  5. Edit in place and watch the numbers move; when you cut for a limit, trim whole sentences rather than shaving words everywhere.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as a word?

Any group of characters separated by spaces or line breaks — "don't" counts as one word, "well-known" counts as one word. This matches how most editors and word processors count.

How is reading time calculated?

Word count ÷ 200 words per minute, the widely used average adult reading speed. A 1,000-word article reads as about 5 minutes.

What are common word limits I should know?

A tweet/X post allows 280 characters. College application essays are often 250–650 words. Blog posts perform best around 1,000–2,000 words, and a typical novel runs 70,000–100,000 words.

How are paragraphs counted — is a blank line required?

No blank line is needed. The counter splits the text at every line break and counts each non-empty piece as a paragraph, so pressing Enter once already starts a new paragraph. Pasted text that arrives as one solid block counts as a single paragraph no matter how long it runs.

Why can the sentence count be higher than the sentences I wrote?

Sentences are counted by splitting on periods, exclamation marks and question marks, and the counter cannot tell a sentence-ending period from the period in an abbreviation or a decimal. Text containing forms like Mr. or a figure like 2.5 will count extra sentence pieces — for ordinary prose without abbreviations the count matches what you would count by hand.

What exactly does Characters (no spaces) remove?

Every whitespace character — spaces, line breaks and tabs — is stripped before counting, and everything else stays, including punctuation. That is why the no-spaces figure is the one to use for specs that price or limit text by its visible characters, while the main Characters figure, spaces included, is the one most form limits mean.
