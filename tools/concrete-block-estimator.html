Concrete Block Estimator

Block walls price out by the unit. Enter the wall dimensions, choose the block, and get the CMU count and mortar bags — the same arithmetic a block supplier does at the counter, waste allowance included.

Block dimensions are nominal: an "8 × 8 × 16" block actually measures 7⅝ × 7⅝ × 15⅝ in, and the ⅜-in mortar joint brings it to the nominal size the math assumes. Openings for doors and windows: subtract their area by shortening the wall length, or run the wall in sections.

Tip: dimensions accept tape-measure entries — try 6' 8 5/16" or 6.5.

How it works

A standard block face is 8 × 16 in nominal — 128 sq in, or 1.125 blocks per sq ft of wall. Half-high blocks (4 in tall) double the courses and the count to 2.25 per sq ft. The estimator multiplies wall area by the blocks-per-sq-ft rate, adds your waste allowance, and counts mortar at about 9 bags of 80-lb premix per 100 standard blocks (12 per 100 half-high — twice the bed joints; 11 per 100 for 12-in block, whose joints carry more mortar). Those yields are the long-standing industry planning figures for ⅜-in joints.

Estimates only. Bond beam, lintel and half blocks, plus vertical rebar and grout fill for reinforced walls, are separate line items your supplier will add from the wall plans. Corner counts assume standard stretcher units throughout.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Blocks = wall area (sq ft) × blocks per sq ft — 1.125 for 8-in-high faces (standard and 12-in-wide block), 2.25 for half-high 4-in block — plus waste. Mortar bags = blocks × bags-per-100 ÷ 100 (9 standard, 12 half-high, 11 wide), the standard premix yields at ⅜-in joints.

Worked example: A 20 × 8 ft foundation wall in standard block: 160 × 1.125 = 180 blocks — 198 with 10% waste — and 18 bags of mortar. The same wall in half-high block needs 395 blocks and 48 bags, because it has twice as many bed joints.

When to use this

  • Building a foundation, retaining stem wall or garage and ordering block by the pallet.
  • Checking a block supplier's count before the truck is loaded.
  • Pricing a CMU garden or shed wall where mortar bags matter as much as blocks.
  • Comparing standard vs half-high block for a stepped or low wall.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Measure the wall length and finished height (courses × 8 in for standard block).
  2. Choose the block: standard 8×8×16 unless the design calls for half-high or 12-in.
  3. Keep waste at 10% for straight walls; drop to 5% for long uninterrupted runs.
  4. Add the result's blocks, then ask the yard about bond-beam and lintel units for openings.
  5. Order mortar bags with the block — one delivery, one fee.

Another worked example

A stepped garden wall, 30 × 10 ft of face, in half-high block for a finer scale: 300 sq ft × 2.25 per sq ft = 675 blocks, 743 with waste. Mortar jumps to 90 bags — half-high block doubles the bed joints, and the joints are where the mortar goes.

Frequently asked questions

How many blocks do I need for a 20 × 8 ft wall?

A standard 8 × 8 × 16 block covers 1.125 per sq ft, so 160 sq ft needs 180 blocks — 198 with a 10% breakage allowance — and about 18 bags of 80-lb mortar mix.

Why do half-high blocks need more mortar per block?

Half-high block doubles the number of courses, so it doubles the bed joints per square foot of wall. Count on roughly 12 bags per 100 blocks instead of 9 — the wall goes up in smaller pieces with more mortar between them.

How many blocks are on a pallet?

Typically 90–120 standard 8 × 8 × 16 blocks per pallet depending on the yard. The default wall here (198 blocks) is about two pallets — worth knowing when you arrange delivery, since a pallet of block weighs over 3,000 lb.

Does wall thickness change the count?

Not the face count: 8-in and 12-in thick blocks share the same 8 × 16 face, so the count per sq ft is identical. Thickness changes weight, strength, mortar per joint slightly, and cost per block — not how many faces cover the wall.

What waste percentage should I use?

Ten percent is the standard planning figure for straight walls. Long walls with few cuts can run 5%; walls with many openings, corners and gable ends justify 12–15%.

How many concrete blocks per pallet should I expect to pay for?

Yards sell by the unit but deliver by the pallet (roughly 90–120 standard blocks). Ask whether partial pallets carry a restocking or breakage fee — ordering to the pallet boundary (as the counts here let you plan) avoids it.

Do I need grout and rebar in the count?

For structural or retaining walls, yes — vertical rebar and grout-filled cells are structural requirements in most codes, figured separately from the block and mortar here. A non-structural garden wall under local height limits often needs neither; check your jurisdiction.
