Dog Age Calculator

The old “times seven” rule is wrong at both ends: dogs race through puppyhood, then age ever more slowly. This calculator uses the modern logarithmic model from UC San Diego research — 16 × ln(dog age) + 31 — adjusted for the puppy months and shown with your dog’s life stage.

How it works

Researchers compared dog and human DNA methylation (the epigenetic clock) and found aging follows a log curve: human years = 16 × ln(dog age) + 31 for dogs over one year. Under one year, development runs to about 15 human years by the first birthday. Size then frames the stage: a 7-year-old Dane is a senior; a 7-year-old Chihuahua is midlife — which is why the flat ×7 rule fails every breed in a different direction.

A population-level model, not a veterinary assessment: breed, health and individual genetics move any specific dog years in either direction. Your vet’s senior-screening advice follows your actual dog, not a formula.

Method & worked example

Method: The modern model comes from epigenetic-clock research (UC San Diego): human years = 16 × ln(dog age) + 31 for dogs past their first year. The first year itself maps to about 15 human years, scaled month by month. Size class then sets the life-stage label — giant breeds reach senior years far earlier than small breeds.

Worked example: A 2-year-old dog is about 42.1 human years — not 14. Age 5 maps to 56.8, and age 10 to 67.8, because the log curve flattens: each later dog year adds fewer human years than the one before.

When to use this

  • Your dog is approaching the age where vets start suggesting senior screening, and you want to know when that actually is for its size.
  • A rescue or rehomed dog has an estimated age and you want its human equivalent for insurance or care planning.
  • Someone quotes the times-seven rule at you and you want the modern figure to answer with.
  • You are marking a dog's birthday in human terms for a card, a post or a family argument about who is really older.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Enter the dog's age in years — fractions work, so six months is 0.5.
  2. Pick the size class by adult weight, not by how big the dog feels on your lap.
  3. Read the human-years figure from the logarithmic model.
  4. Check the life-stage label underneath it; for big breeds it changes years earlier than for small ones.
  5. Use the times-seven figure shown for contrast only — it is there to retire, not to use.

Another worked example

An 8-year-old large-breed dog comes out at 64.3 human years on the log model — the old times-seven rule would have said 56, undershooting by more than eight years. The size class matters to the label: at 8, a large breed is classed as Senior, because large breeds cross that threshold at 8, while a small dog with the exact same 64.3 figure is still classed as an Adult. It is a population model, not a veterinary judgement on any individual dog.

Frequently asked questions

How old is a 2-year-old dog in human years?

About 42 — not 14. The log model front-loads aging: year one is roughly 15 human years, year two lands near 42, and each later year adds fewer. By age 5 the figure is about 57, by 10 about 68.

Why is “multiply by 7” wrong?

It implies linear aging, and dogs do not age linearly. A 1-year-old dog is reproductively mature — closer to a 15-year-old human than a 7-year-old — while a 15-year-old dog is not 105 in any meaningful sense. The ×7 rule survives because it is easy, not because it is right.

Does size change the answer?

The human-years figure is the same curve for all sizes in this model, but the life stage is not: small dogs are puppies to about 1 year and seniors from 11; giant breeds hit senior at 6–7. Same number, very different dog — the label tells the truer story.

What about cats?

Different curve entirely — cats map roughly 15 human years in year one and about 24 by year two, then roughly 4 per year after. This calculator is dogs only; do not run a cat through it and panic.

How do I enter a puppy that is only a few months old?

Use a fraction of a year: 0.5 is six months, 0.25 is about three months. Under one year the model scales the first year's roughly 15 human years by age, so a six-month puppy reads 7.5 human years. Development is fast down there, which is why the same six months in an adult dog barely moves its figure at all.

Does the breed itself change the result, or only the size?

Only size is an input. The human-years curve is one line for every dog, and size class changes just the life-stage label. In real life, breed, genetics, weight and health all shift how an individual dog ages — two same-size dogs can be years apart in condition — so treat the stage label as a prompt for a conversation with your vet, not a diagnosis.
