How it works

Imperial: BMI = 703 × weight (lbs) ÷ height (in)2. Metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)2. Categories follow CDC cutoffs: under 18.5 underweight, 18.5–24.9 healthy, 25–29.9 overweight, 30+ obese.

BMI is a screening measure, not a diagnosis — it doesn't account for muscle mass, bone density, or body composition. Talk to your doctor for personal health advice.

Formula & worked example

Formula: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)², or weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². Categories: under 18.5 underweight, 18.5–24.9 healthy, 25–29.9 overweight, 30+ obese.

Worked example: At 180 lb and 5′10″ (70 in): 703 × 180 ÷ 4,900 = 25.8 — just inside the overweight band.

When to use this

  • A quick screening number for a health form, sport or insurance questionnaire.
  • You are tracking weight change and want the category context, not just the scale.
  • You are setting a goal weight and want to know which BMI it corresponds to.
  • A clinician mentioned BMI and you want to see where the bands sit for your height.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Choose imperial or metric.
  2. Enter height and weight as they are today.
  3. Read the BMI and its band.
  4. Use the healthy-range line to see the weights that correspond to BMI 18.5–24.9 at your height.
  5. If you train seriously, pair this with a body-fat estimate — BMI cannot see muscle.

Another worked example

At 5′6″ and 160 lb, BMI is 25.8 — just inside the 'overweight' band (25–29.9). The same person at 148 lb would read 23.9, inside the healthy band, which shows how few pounds separate the categories at this height — and why the trend matters more than any single reading.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?

The CDC defines a healthy BMI as 18.5 to 24.9 for adults. Athletes with high muscle mass may score above 25 while still being healthy.

How accurate is BMI?

It's a quick population-level screen, not a body-fat measurement. Two people with the same BMI can have very different health profiles.

Should I convert my units first?

No — just pick Imperial or Metric above and enter your numbers as-is. The tool converts behind the scenes.

Why does BMI call muscular people overweight?

BMI is weight divided by height squared — it has no way to know what the weight is made of. A lean athlete at 5′10″ and 200 lb scores about 28.7. That is a known limitation, which is why waist size and body-fat percentage are the follow-up checks.

Is BMI used for children?

Not this adult scale. Children and teens are assessed on percentile curves by age and sex — a pediatric standard this calculator deliberately does not apply.
