How it works

Core volume scales with block width. The planning figures used across the industry (and by ready-mix drivers): filling every core takes about 0.55 cu yd per 100 blocks for 6-in block, 1.00 for 8-in, 1.45 for 10-in and 1.95 for 12-in. Filling every other core halves the per-core share; every third takes a third. Bags assume an 80-lb bag yields about 0.6 cu ft mixed — which is why anything past a yard is a ready-mix conversation, not a bag conversation.

Estimates only. Actual fill runs higher with broken webs, wide joints and spillage — order a quarter-yard over on truck pours. Grout for structural fill is a specified material (fine grout, high slump); plain bagged concrete mix is not its equal in reinforced work — follow your engineer.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Fill yards = blocks ÷ 100 × the planning rate for the block size (6 in: 0.55, 8 in: 1.00, 10 in: 1.45, 12 in: 1.95 cu yd per 100 blocks) × the pattern fraction (every core 1.0, every other 0.5, every third ⅓). 80-lb bags at 0.6 cu ft mixed each.

Worked example: 198 eight-inch blocks with every core filled: 1.98 cu yd of grout — about 90 eighty-pound bags, which is why core fill is normally a truck pour.

When to use this

  • You are grouting CMU cores and ordering from a ready-mix plant.
  • A wall spec calls for filled cores at set spacing and you need the yards.
  • You are deciding between bags and a truck for a small fill job.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Get the block count (the Concrete Block Estimator counts any wall).
  2. Pick the block width — 8 in is the residential standard.
  3. Pick the fill pattern from the structural drawings.
  4. Order the yards shown, plus a quarter-yard cushion on truck pours.

Frequently asked questions

How much concrete fills 100 8-inch blocks?

About 1.00 cu yd with every core filled — 0.50 yd filling every other core, 0.33 every third. In bags that full yard is roughly 45 eighty-pound bags, which is why core fill is normally a ready-mix or grout-pump job.

How many bags to fill 198 blocks?

The default wall here — 198 eight-inch blocks, every core: 1.98 cu yd, or about 90 eighty-pound bags. Two yards from a truck costs less than the bags and pours in minutes instead of a weekend.

Which cores actually need filling?

Cores holding vertical rebar, cores at openings and corners, and bond-beam courses — the pattern comes from the structural drawings. Decorative and garden walls are often filled only where steel sits; a retaining or load-bearing wall may spec every core.

Grout or concrete in the cores?

Grout — it is mixed wet and fine specifically to flow around rebar and fill webs completely. Standard concrete with coarse aggregate bridges and leaves voids. This estimator sizes the volume; the material choice belongs to the spec.

Does the mortar count change the fill?

Barely — joints sit between blocks, not in cores. The block estimator's mortar figure and this fill figure are separate purchases for the same wall.

How much grout for bond beams?

A filled bond-beam course takes roughly the all-cores rate for one course's worth of blocks. Add a course-equivalent per beam course to the vertical-fill figure when the wall has both.

Can I fill cores with a standard concrete truck mix?

Plants supply a dedicated grout/fill mix with smaller aggregate and high slump for core filling — order it by that name. Standard ¾-in aggregate mix bridges in 8-in cores and leaves voids around the steel.
