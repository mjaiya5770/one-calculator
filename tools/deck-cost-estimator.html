Deck Cost Estimator

Deck quotes swing wildly because "a deck" hides the material, the railing and the stairs inside one number. This estimator separates them: decking band by material, railing by the lineal foot, stairs as their own line — so you can see exactly what a quote is charging for.

Railing is priced on three open sides (length + both widths — the house side needs none) at $45–$75 per lineal foot installed; a standard stair flight at $1,200–$2,400. Ranges are typical 2026 US installed rates; the regional note below applies.

How it works

The deck platform prices per square foot by decking material: pressure-treated $45–$65, cedar $55–$80, composite $65–$100, PVC $75–$110, ipe $90–$140 per sq ft installed (structure included). Then the two lines quotes bury: railing at $45–$75 per lineal foot across the open sides, and stairs at $1,200–$2,400 a flight. On the default 16 × 12 composite deck the platform is $12,480–$19,200 of the $15,480–$24,600 total — railing and stairs are a fifth of the job, which is why two "same size" quotes can differ by thousands honestly.

Estimates only, typical US ranges — real quotes vary ±20% by region and season, and more for second-storey builds, steep sites and complex shapes. This is a budgeting compass for comparing quotes, not a bid; any quote far outside the band deserves its line items explained.

Method & worked example

Method: Total = platform (area × material band: pressure-treated $45–65, cedar $55–80, composite $65–100, PVC $75–110, ipe $90–140 per sq ft) + railing (open-side lineal ft × $45–75) + stairs ($1,200–2,400 per flight).

Worked example: A 16 × 12 ft composite deck with railing (40 lineal ft) and stairs: $12,480–$19,200 platform + $1,800–$3,000 railing + $1,200–$2,400 stairs = $15,480–$24,600.

When to use this

  • You are budgeting a new deck and quotes differ by thousands.
  • You are deciding between pressure-treated and composite with real totals.
  • A quote bundles railing and stairs invisibly and you want them itemised.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Enter deck length and width.
  2. Pick the decking material.
  3. Toggle railing (three open sides) and stairs to match the design.
  4. Compare any quote against the three itemised lines, not just the total.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 16 × 12 deck cost?

In composite with railing and stairs: $15,480–$24,600 installed, mid-band about $20,040. The same deck in pressure-treated runs $8,040–$12,600 at 12 × 10 — scale the platform band by your own footprint; material choice is the largest single lever you control.

How much does a 12 × 10 pressure-treated deck cost?

$8,040–$12,600 installed with railing and stairs: $5,400–$7,800 of platform, $1,440–$2,400 of railing (32 lineal ft), $1,200–$2,400 of stairs. Small decks cost more per square foot than big ones — the railing and stair lines do not shrink proportionally.

Is composite worth the extra cost?

On upkeep math, often yes over 15+ years: no staining every 1–2 years (the Deck Stain Estimator prices that habit at roughly 3 gallons and a weekend per round), no rot-board replacements. The platform premium over pressure-treated is about $20–$35 per sq ft.

Why are railing and stairs separate lines?

Because they are separate work: 40 lineal ft of railing on the default deck is $1,800–$3,000 — as much as the entire platform in cheap material. Quotes that roll them in silently cannot be compared; quotes that list them can.

What adds cost fastest?

Height (second-storey decks need taller posts, more railing, more engineering), curves and diagonal layouts (labour multipliers), and built-ins — benches, pergolas, lighting. Each is lovely; each is a line item.

Does a permit add much to deck cost?

Permit fees typically run $150–$600 depending on jurisdiction and project value — small next to the build, and the inspection protects the railing and footing decisions that matter.

What about a second-story deck?

Add for taller posts, doubled railing exposure, stair length and often engineering: real quotes land 25–50% above the same footprint at grade. This estimator prices grade-level decks; treat its output as the floor for elevated designs.
