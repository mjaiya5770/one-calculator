How it works

Installed per-square-foot bands: gravel $2–$4.50, asphalt $5–$9, concrete $8–$14, pavers $14–$24. The bands are wide because base work is destiny: a driveway is a structure (excavation, compacted aggregate base, then surface), and quotes differ most in how much structure they include. Tear-out and disposal of an old drive adds about $2 per sq ft. Gravel's band buys a properly based, crowned gravel drive — not a load of stone dumped on dirt, which is a $500 lesson people buy once.

Estimates only, typical US ranges. Drainage corrections, steep grades, permits and winter scheduling move real quotes. Compare quotes on base depth and surface thickness, not just totals — the cheapest bid is often the thinnest structure, and driveways announce that in year three.

Method & worked example

Method: Total = area × material band (gravel $2–4.50, asphalt $5–9, concrete $8–14, pavers $14–24 per sq ft) + optional tear-out and disposal at $2 per sq ft.

Worked example: A 20 × 20 ft asphalt driveway on bare ground: 400 sq ft × $5–9 = $2,000–$3,600 installed, mid-band $2,800.

When to use this

  • You are replacing a driveway and comparing surface materials.
  • A paving quote needs a per-square-foot sanity check.
  • You are budgeting tear-out of the old drive into the project.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Measure length × width, folding in aprons and turnarounds.
  2. Pick the surface material.
  3. Toggle tear-out if the old driveway must come out.
  4. Compare quotes per square foot against the band, and ask about base depth.

A long concrete drive: 16 × 40 ft = 640 sq ft × $8–14 = $5,120–$8,960 installed, mid-band $7,040. In asphalt the same footprint would run $3,200–$5,760 — on long driveways the surface choice is a four-figure decision per material step.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 20 × 20 asphalt driveway cost?

About $2,000–$3,600 installed (400 sq ft in the $5–$9 band) on bare ground; add roughly $800 if the old drive must come out first. The same footprint in concrete runs $3,200–$5,600; in pavers, $5,600–$9,600; in gravel, $800–$1,800.

How much for a big 16 × 40 concrete driveway?

640 sq ft: $5,120–$8,960 installed. Long driveways are where per-foot pricing compounds — and where a gravel or asphalt surface saves five figures against pavers. Measure the apron and any turnaround into the area.

Asphalt or concrete — which is better value?

Asphalt wins on install price and speed (drive on it in days, sealcoat every few years at modest cost); concrete wins on lifespan and zero sealcoating, and shrugs off fuel drips. Over 20 years the gap narrows; over 5, asphalt is clearly cheaper. The Asphalt Estimator prices the material tonnage if you are comparing a DIY-adjacent route.

Why is the tear-out extra?

Because the old driveway has to go somewhere: breaking, loading and legal disposal of 10+ tons of material is real work and real dump fees. Any quote silent on tear-out is either assuming bare ground or planning a surprise.

Can I just pave over the old driveway?

An asphalt overlay on sound concrete or asphalt exists (that is the 2-in resurfacing option in the Asphalt Estimator) — but over a cracked, settled base it telegraphs every flaw within a season. Overlay sound surfaces only.

How thick should a concrete driveway be?

Four inches over a compacted base is the residential standard, 5–6 in where heavy vehicles park. A quote saving money at 3 in is selling you a future crack map — thickness and base are the two lines to interrogate.

When can I drive on a new concrete driveway?

Typically 7 days for cars (concrete reaches most of its working strength by then), longer for heavy trucks. Asphalt is faster: 24–72 hours in most weather.
