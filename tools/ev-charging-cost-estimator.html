How it works

Energy at the wheels: miles × kWh-per-100-mi ÷ 100. Energy at the wall — the stuff you pay for — is that × (1 + losses): 1,000 miles at 30 kWh/100mi is 300 kWh at the wheels but 336 kWh on the meter. Cost is wall kWh × your rate. Gas for the same miles: miles ÷ mpg × pump price. Cost per mile is the honest comparison line: the defaults land at 5.4¢ electric vs 11.5¢ gas.

Estimates only. Time-of-use rates can halve home charging cost on an overnight schedule — check your utility's EV plan. Public DC fast charging often costs 2–3× the home rate; this estimator prices home charging, which is where most miles actually come from. Cold weather raises consumption 15–30% — winter drivers should test 35–40 kWh/100mi.

Formula & worked example

Formula: kWh at the wall = miles × kWh-per-100-miles ÷ 100 × (1 + charging losses %). Cost = wall kWh × rate. Gas comparison = miles ÷ mpg × gas price. Defaults carry 12% losses — the meter sees more than the battery keeps.

Worked example: 1,000 miles/mo at 30 kWh/100mi and $0.16/kWh: 336 kWh at the wall, $53.76/mo against $115.00 of 30-mpg gas at $3.45 — $734.88 saved per year.

When to use this

  • You are EV-shopping and want the fuel bill before the car.
  • Your first electric bill after buying an EV needs explaining.
  • You are comparing your real rate and mileage against the gas car it replaced.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Enter your true monthly miles.
  2. Enter the car's consumption (window sticker kWh/100mi).
  3. Enter your home electric rate from a bill.
  4. Read the wall cost (losses in) against the gas comparison.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to charge an EV for 1,000 miles a month?

At 30 kWh/100mi, $0.16/kWh and 12% charging losses: $53.76 a month — $645.12 a year. The same 1,000 miles at 30 mpg and $3.45 gas cost $115.00 a month, so the default saving is $734.88 a year.

What is the cost per mile of an EV vs gas?

Defaults here: 5.4¢ per mile electric (at the wall, losses in) against 11.5¢ gas. A more efficient driver (1,500 mi/mo at 28 kWh/100mi, $0.20/kWh) pays $94.08 against $154.29 in a 35-mpg car at $3.60 — the ratio survives most rate combinations.

Why count charging losses?

Because the meter does. Chargers, cabling and battery chemistry waste 10–15% as heat; tools that skip losses understate your bill by exactly that much. Twelve percent is the planning figure used here — change it if your charger reports its own.

What does a full charge cost?

Battery size × (1 + losses) × rate: a 60-kWh pack from empty at $0.16 costs about $10.75 at the wall. You rarely arrive at zero — the monthly view in this estimator is the budget-honest way to look at it.

Is public charging still cheaper than gas?

Usually, but not always: at 2.5× a $0.16 home rate the electric mile is about 13¢ — slightly worse than 30-mpg gas at $3.45. Home charging is where the EV economy lives; road-trip fast charging is a convenience purchase.

What does Level 1 vs Level 2 change in cost?

Almost nothing per kWh — the rate is the rate. Level 2 (240V) is slightly more efficient (lower losses) and much faster; Level 1 adds convenience limits, not meaningful cost differences.

How much does an EV add to a home's electric bill in winter?

Cold-soaked batteries and cabin heat raise consumption 15–30%. Re-run this estimator at 35–40 kWh/100mi for your winter months to budget the seasonal bump honestly.
