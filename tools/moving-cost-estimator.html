How it works

Estimates use national averages: professional movers charge a base by home size plus per-mile for long hauls (local moves under 100 miles use an hourly-style flat rate). DIY combines a truck rental base, per-mile rate, and fuel. Results show realistic low–high ranges.

Actual quotes vary by season, stairs, packing services, and demand. Summer and month-end moves cost more.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Movers are modelled as a base range by home size (a 2-bedroom base is $1,400–$2,200): a local move under 100 miles costs 55% of the base, while a long-distance move adds $2.20–$3.40 per mile. The DIY figure is a truck base plus a per-mile rate plus fuel at about 10 mpg.

Worked example: A 2-bedroom move of 500 miles: movers come to $2,500–$3,900 against a DIY truck estimate of $704–$893. Done locally instead, the same movers would run about $770–$1,210.

When to use this

  • You are planning a move and need a first budget range before asking any company for a quote.
  • You are torn between hiring movers and renting a truck, and want the honest price gap for your size of home and distance.
  • A job in another state is on the table and the relocation cost is part of judging the offer.
  • You are moving locally and want to know why the estimate changes so much under a certain distance.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Select your home size, from studio to five-bedroom — in this estimator it sets the base price ranges for both movers and truck rental.
  2. Enter the distance in miles, door to door.
  3. Read the professional movers range, the DIY truck range, and the saving the tool shows for going DIY, which brackets the gap from both ends.
  4. Check the move type line: under 100 miles the estimator treats the move as local; at 100 or more it prices a long-distance move, so a distance near that line is worth running both ways.
  5. Use the range to budget, then get binding quotes — season, stairs, packing and heavy items all move a real quote inside or outside it.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to hire movers or do it yourself?

DIY is almost always cheaper in dollars — but factor in your time, risk of injury or damage, and whether friends will actually show up.

What counts as a long-distance move?

Generally over 100 miles (or crossing state lines). Long-distance moves are priced by weight and distance; local moves are usually hourly.

When is the cheapest time to move?

Mid-month, mid-week, and fall through winter. Avoid June–August and the last weekend of any month.

What is not included in these estimates?

Packing services and materials, stairs and long-carry fees, piano or safe handling, storage between homes, and full-value insurance beyond basic carrier liability. On the DIY side, your own time, hired loading help and a second vehicle for the car are also outside the figure. Any of these can add hundreds to either route, so ask for them as named lines in a real quote.

Is the number a mover quotes me binding?

Not always. A non-binding estimate can change with the actual weight or time on the day; a binding or not-to-exceed estimate fixes the ceiling in writing. Compare the type of quote, not just the number, and be wary of a long-distance quote far below every other — the difference tends to reappear at delivery. This estimator's ranges are planning figures, not quotes.

How far ahead should I book, and does timing change the price?

Four to eight weeks ahead is typical, and timing changes price a lot: summer, month-end and weekends are peak, and both movers and truck rentals price to that demand. The same home and distance moved mid-week in a quiet month can land near the bottom of the ranges shown here, while a late-June Saturday pushes toward the top.
