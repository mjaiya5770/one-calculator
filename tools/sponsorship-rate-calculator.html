Sponsorship Rate Calculator

Brands are not buying your follower count — they are buying views, attention and trust in a niche. Enter your average views and engagement and get a low/mid/high asking range with every step of the math visible, so you can defend the number in a negotiation.

The multipliers are editorial planning values, labeled as such — there is no official rate card for influence. The base is a cost per 1,000 views ($20 low / $25 mid / $35 high) in line with what mid-size creators commonly report charging in November 2026. Engagement adjusts the result: above 5% adds 15%, under 2% subtracts 15%, because brands pay for attention, not impressions alone. Usage rights (the brand running your video as their ad) are a separate, chargeable thing — never give them away in the base price.

How it works

Rate = average views ÷ 1,000 × base rate per 1,000 views × niche multiplier × content-type multiplier × engagement adjustment × usage uplift. Worked through: 50,000 views × $25 per 1,000 × 1.2 (tech) × 1.0 (dedicated) = $1,500 mid. The low and high lines swap the base rate ($20 / $35) so you can see the full negotiating range. Dedicated content costs the brand your entire audience's attention for a whole video; an integration rents a segment of it, and a mention rents a moment — the multipliers price exactly that difference.

Editorial planning ranges, not market guarantees. Rates actually paid depend on your audience's country mix, buying power, past conversion results for brands, exclusivity demands and season (Q4 budgets are bigger). Use the mid as your opening ask, the low as your walk-away floor, and let the brand's extras — revisions, exclusivity, usage — move the price up, never down.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Rate = average views ÷ 1,000 × base rate per 1,000 views ($20 low / $25 mid / $35 high) × niche multiplier × content-type multiplier × engagement adjustment (×1.15 above 5%, ×0.85 below 2%) × usage-rights uplift. Every multiplier is an editorial planning value, shown on the page.

Worked example: 50,000 views, tech (×1.2), dedicated (×1.0), 4% engagement (×1.0): 50 × $25 × 1.2 = $1,500 mid, $1,200 low, $2,100 high.

When to use this

  • A brand emails 'what are your rates?' and you need a defensible number today
  • You are moving from gifted-product deals to paid deals and need an anchor
  • An agency offers a package across several posts and you want the per-post sanity check
  • Your views grew and your old rate no longer matches your reach

How to use it, step by step

  1. Enter your median views across the last 10 posts — not your best video.
  2. Set your real engagement rate from your analytics, not a guess.
  3. Pick your niche and the content type the brand is asking for.
  4. Add the usage uplift if they want to run your content as ads.
  5. Quote the mid as your ask, hold the low as your private floor.
  6. Trade scope (revisions, exclusivity length) before you ever trade price.

Another worked example

An Instagram beauty creator averaging 20,000 views per reel, 6% engagement, quoting an integration: 20 × $25 × 1.1 (beauty) × 0.6 (integration) × 1.15 (engagement above 5%) = $380 mid, with a $304–$531 range. Small absolute numbers at this scale are normal for integrations — the lever that moves them is average views, which is why one strong series is worth more to your rate card than a posting streak.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I charge for a sponsored video with 50,000 views?

On this model, a dedicated tech video at 4% engagement prices at $1,200–$2,100 with a $1,500 mid. The same views in personal finance reach a $1,875 mid; as a 60-second integration inside a longer video, the tech mid drops to $900.

Why do brands care about average views instead of subscribers?

Because subscribers do not watch — views are what the brand is buying. A 500,000-subscriber channel averaging 20,000 views sells 20,000 views. Send brands your last 10 videos' view counts, not your subscriber screenshot.

What is a fair charge for usage rights or whitelisting?

Commonly 20–50% on top of the base rate for a defined period (30–90 days), priced separately and time-limited. Indefinite or perpetual usage should cost far more — you are licensing your face and voice as their ad creative. This calculator adds 30% when you select it.

Should engagement rate change my price?

Yes. A 6% engagement rate means your audience acts on what you post, which is what the brand is really buying; under 2% usually means passive or bought reach. The ±15% adjustment here is deliberately modest — proven conversion history moves real rates far more than any formula.

Is it better to quote low, mid or high?

Quote the mid-to-high with the math ready to show. Brands expect negotiation; opening at your low means settling below it. Anchor on value delivered (views in a niche with buying power) and trade scope — fewer revisions, shorter exclusivity — instead of trading price.

How do sponsorships compare with ad revenue?

One dedicated sponsorship at the mid rate for 50,000 views ($1,500) equals roughly 100,000 long-form views at a $14 RPM, or about 30 million rewarded TikTok views at a $0.05 effective rate. For most creators under a million subscribers, sponsorships are the business; ads are the bonus.

Should I publish a rate card?

A range, yes; a single price, no. Publish 'integrations from $X, dedicated videos from $Y' to filter out lowball offers, and keep the top of the range private so strong-fit brands can still surprise you. Anchoring high with the math ready beats defending a low published price.

What if a brand offers product instead of payment?

Price the product at its retail value and compare honestly: gifted product worth $200 against a $1,500 mid rate is a 13% offer. Product-only deals can make sense for products you would buy anyway, early on, for portfolio pieces — but they are a discount, not a different currency.
