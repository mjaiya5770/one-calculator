How it works

Your monthly payment uses the standard amortization formula: M = P × r(1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r the monthly rate, and n the number of payments. Total interest is all payments minus principal; the origination fee is added on top for your true total cost.

Estimates only — not a loan offer. Actual terms depend on credit, revenue history, collateral, and lender policies.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Monthly payment M = P × r(1+r)n ÷ ((1+r)n − 1), with r the monthly interest rate and n the term in months. Total interest = payment × n − principal, and the total borrowing cost adds the origination fee to all the payments.

Worked example: Borrow $100,000 at 8% for 5 years: the payment is $2,028 a month and total interest is $21,658. With a $1,500 origination fee, the all-in cost of the loan is $123,158.

When to use this

  • You have a term sheet for equipment, a vehicle or working capital and want the real monthly payment before cash flow commits.
  • A lender quotes the rate but buries an origination fee in the closing paperwork, and you want the all-in cost in one number.
  • You are choosing between a short term that strains monthly cash flow and a long term that quietly doubles the interest.
  • The bank wants a decision this week and you need to test the offer against a second lender's numbers side by side.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Enter the amount you need to borrow, not the amount a lender offered to stretch to.
  2. Enter the annual rate from the term sheet.
  3. Pick the term in years — the options run 1, 3, 5, 7 and 10.
  4. Add the origination fee in dollars; if the lender quotes it as a percentage, multiply it out first.
  5. Compare the total cost line (payments plus fee) across offers, not just the monthly payment.

Another worked example

A bakery borrows $60,000 at 9% for 3 years with a $900 origination fee. The payment is $1,907.98 a month across 36 payments, total interest comes to $8,687.42, and the payments add up to $68,687.42. Add the fee and the loan's all-in cost is $69,587.42 — the figure to weigh against what the new equipment earns, not the $60,000 headline.

Frequently asked questions

How do business loan rates work?

Rates are set from your credit profile, business revenue, time in business, and collateral. Stronger profiles earn lower rates; riskier borrowers pay more. Fixed-rate loans keep the same payment for the whole term.

Shorter or longer term — which is better?

Shorter terms mean higher monthly payments but far less total interest. Longer terms ease cash flow but cost much more overall. Compare both above and pick the shortest term your monthly budget can handle.

SBA loan vs. bank loan — what's the difference?

SBA-backed loans typically offer lower rates and longer terms but take weeks or months to close with heavy paperwork. Conventional bank loans close faster but usually carry higher rates and stricter requirements. This calculator works for either once you have the numbers.

What does the origination fee change, and what does it leave alone?

It leaves the monthly payment alone — the payment is worked out from the amount, rate and term only. What the fee changes is the true cost: it is added on top of all the payments in the total cost line, so two loans with identical payments can still differ by thousands once their fees are counted.

Which charges count as an origination fee here?

Any one-time charge the lender takes for making the loan: origination or underwriting fees, documentation and processing charges, and discount points converted to dollars. Ongoing costs like insurance or a required deposit account are not origination fees — leave those out and judge them separately.

Why compare total cost instead of the monthly payment?

Because a longer term can make an expensive loan look cheap month to month while adding years of interest. The payment tells you whether the business can carry the loan; the total cost tells you what the loan actually charges for the money. An offer should win on both, or you should know exactly which one you are trading away.
