For a rectangular floor or roof plane, area alone is honest. For walls, subtract the big openings. For roofs, remember the true sloped area is bigger than the footprint — the Roofing Estimator works that factor out.

How it works

One standard sheet covers 32 sq ft (4 × 8). Sheets = area × (1 + allowance) ÷ sheet coverage, rounded up — partial sheets do not exist at the yard. The allowance is not sloppiness: staggered joints on floors and roofs mean every other row starts with a half sheet, hips and valleys cut diagonals, and one forklift-damaged corner deletes a corner of a sheet. Ten percent suits rectangles; go 15% on cut-up roofs and gabled walls.

Estimates only. Sheet count is not a layout: stagger joints, keep end joints on framing, and mind the span rating stamped on the sheet (a ⅞-in subfloor sheet and a ½-in sheathing sheet are not interchangeable, whatever the count says).

Formula & worked example

Formula: Sheets = ceil(area × (1 + cutting allowance) ÷ sheet coverage). A standard 4 × 8 sheet covers 32 sq ft; 4 × 9 covers 36, 4 × 10 covers 40, 4 × 12 covers 48.

Worked example: 500 sq ft with a 10% allowance: 550 ÷ 32 rounds up to 18 sheets, covering 576 sq ft. A 1,000 sq ft roof at a 15% allowance takes 36 sheets.

When to use this

  • You are sheathing a floor, roof or wall and ordering sheet goods.
  • You are building shop cabinets and converting a cut list into sheets.
  • You want the count with layout waste included, not the raw division.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Enter the area to cover (for roofs, use true sloped area).
  2. Pick the sheet size your yard stocks.
  3. Set the allowance: 10% rectangles, 15% cut-up shapes.
  4. Order the sheet count shown.

Another worked example

A 1,000 sq ft roof deck at a 15% allowance (hips and valleys eat sheets): 1,150 ÷ 32 rounds up to 36 sheets of 4×8, covering 1,152 sq ft. The six sheets above the raw 32-by-division are where the staggered joints and diagonal cuts live.

Frequently asked questions

How many sheets of plywood do I need for 500 sq ft?

Eighteen 4×8 sheets: 500 sq ft with a 10% cutting allowance is 550, and 550 ÷ 32 rounds up to 18 — covering 576 sq ft, with the extra half-sheet being your layout cuts.

How many sheets for a 1,000 sq ft roof?

Thirty-six at a 15% allowance (cut-up shapes and hips eat more), 35 at 10%. Roof decks also want clips and edge support on wide rafter spacing — small hardware, separate line at the yard.

What thickness do I need?

Subfloor: ¾ in tongue-and-groove is the standard (⅞ under tile). Roof sheathing: ½ or ⅝ in depending on rafter spacing and snow load. Walls: ½ in typical, ⅜ only where the code and bracing plan allow. Thickness changes price, not the count.

Plywood or OSB?

OSB is cheaper, flatter and fine for sheathing and subfloors kept dry during construction; plywood shrugs off jobsite weather and holds fasteners better at edges. The sheet count is identical either way.

Why not just divide area by 32?

Because 500 ÷ 32 = 15.6, and sheet 16 is where your staggered joints and cut-offs live. Buying the raw quotient guarantees a second trip with the last row half-finished.

🏠 Sheathing a roof?

If those sheets are a roof deck, the Roofing Estimator starts from the house footprint and pitch and counts the whole roof — bundles, underlayment and nails too.

How many sheets for a 12 × 16 shed floor?

192 sq ft: at 10% allowance, 7 sheets of 4×8 — six cover the rectangle exactly and the seventh covers the staggered-joint halves. The estimator's 500 sq ft default shows the same logic at room scale: 18 sheets.

Can I mix thicknesses on one subfloor?

No — thickness steps telegraph through finished flooring and squeak at the seams. One thickness per plane; transition strips handle the room-to-room changes.
