Trip Cost Estimator

Vacations are under-budgeted by exactly the lines nobody writes down. This estimator counts all of them — transport per person, lodging per night, food and activities per person per day — and splits the total per traveler.

Days = nights + 1, because a 5-night trip has 6 days of eating in it. Lodging is charged per night for the whole party (add a second room's rate into the nightly figure if your party needs two).

How it works

Transport = per-person fare × travelers. Lodging = nightly rate × nights. Food and activities = per-person-per-day × travelers × days (nights + 1) — the arrival and departure days still eat, which is the most-missed line in home-made trip budgets. Add the fixed extras once. Per-person cost divides by travelers, and it falls as the party grows because lodging and fixed costs are shared while fares and food are not — the estimator makes that split visible, which is the whole arithmetic of group trips.

Estimates only — your prices, your trip. Resort fees, baggage charges and rental-car upsells live in the "other" line if you feed them in; travel insurance at 4–6% of trip cost is a common and defensible addition there.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Total = transport per person × travelers + lodging per night × nights + (food + activities) per person per day × travelers × days + fixed extras, where days = nights + 1. Per person = total ÷ travelers.

Worked example: Two travelers, 5 nights: transport $900, lodging $800, food $840 and activities $480 across 6 days, plus $200 fixed = $3,220 total — $1,610 per person. Note food nearly equals the flights; that is typical, not a mistake.

When to use this

  • Budgeting a vacation and wanting the true total before booking anything.
  • Splitting a group trip fairly, with shared and per-person costs separated.
  • Comparing two trip lengths or party sizes on honest like-for-like totals.
  • Setting a savings target for a planned trip (pair with the Savings Goal Calculator).

How to use it, step by step

  1. Set travelers and nights — days for food are nights + 1 automatically.
  2. Enter transport per person (fare or their share of driving costs).
  3. Enter the whole-party nightly lodging rate (both rooms if two).
  4. Set food and activity budgets per person per day from the trip's style.
  5. Add fixed extras: transfers, parking, insurance, fees — the forgotten lines.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 5-night trip for two cost?

On the defaults — $450 flights each, $160 a night lodging, $70 food and $40 activities per person per day over 6 days, $200 of extras — the total is $3,220, or $1,610 per person. Food ($840) nearly matches the flights ($900), which surprises most first-time budgeters.

Why count nights + 1 days of food?

A 5-night trip spans 6 calendar days, and you eat on all of them. Counting only 5 days of food under-budgets the default trip by $220 — small money, but it is exactly the class of leak that makes vacations feel mysteriously over budget.

How much should I budget per day for food on vacation?

US domestic planning figures: $50–$80 per person per day for mixed cooking and eating out, $100+ for restaurant-led city trips, $30–$45 for self-catering-heavy ones. The field takes your real number — a grocery run on day one changes it more than any estimate.

Does a bigger group make a trip cheaper per person?

The shared lines, yes: on these defaults a solo traveler pays $2,110 for the same trip that costs each member of a pair $1,610, because the $160 room and $200 of extras stop being shared. The solo example in the detail section shows a 3-night version at $1,180 all-in.

What do people most often forget to budget?

Airport transfers and parking, baggage fees, resort fees (often $25–$50 a night, charged at checkout), tips, and the food on travel days. They are all "other fixed costs" here — a $200 default that real trips usually argue should be $300.

How much spending money should I add on top?

Souvenirs and shopping are personal, not logistical — travellers commonly add 10–20% of the trip total. The 'other fixed costs' field absorbs it here, or add it mentally to the per-person figure before setting the savings target.

Road trip instead of flying — what changes?

Transport per person becomes (fuel + tolls + parking) ÷ travelers: a 1,200-mile round trip at 30 mpg and $3.60/gal is $144 of fuel for the whole car. Enter that divided figure per person — the estimator's split math handles the rest identically.
