Carpet Estimator

Carpet is the one floor covering sold by the square yard and delivered on a roll — so the real questions are yards, roll length and seams. Two rooms, one waste allowance, straight answers.

Padding (underlay) covers the same area as the carpet — the estimator sizes both from the same number. Rooms wider than the roll need seams; the 10% allowance absorbs the extra width that seaming eats.

How it works

Add the room areas, apply the waste allowance, and divide by 9 — carpet is priced per square yard (9 sq ft). Roll length is the with-waste area divided by the roll width: carpet comes off a 12-ft roll, so a 198 sq ft order is a 16.5-ft cut. Padding is sold in rolls and bundles rated by square yards and simply matches the carpet area. The waste figure does real work in carpet: pile direction must run the same way in every piece, so offcuts cannot always rotate into another corner the way tile or flooring offcuts can.

Estimates only. Patterned carpet needs an extra allowance for pattern matching — 15–20% is the installer norm — and stairs are priced per step by most installers rather than by area. Get the installer's own measurement before ordering; theirs is the number the warranty stands behind.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Square yards = (room areas summed × (1 + waste)) ÷ 9. Padding matches the same square-yard figure. Roll length = area with waste ÷ roll width (12 ft standard), which is the cut length to ask the warehouse for.

Worked example: A 12 × 15 ft room: 180 sq ft, 198 with 10% waste — exactly 22.00 sq yd of carpet and padding, cut as 16.5 ft off a 12-ft roll. Add a 10 × 12 ft second room and the order becomes 45.47 sq yd.

When to use this

  • Ordering carpet for a room or two and needing square yards, not square feet.
  • Comparing retailer quotes — one in sq ft, one in sq yd — on equal footing.
  • Budgeting padding with the carpet instead of as a checkout surprise.
  • A landlord refreshing units between tenants, room by room.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Measure each room's length and width (into closets the carpet will enter).
  2. Enter a second room if it shares the same carpet and dye lot.
  3. Keep waste at 10% for plain carpet, 15–20% for patterns.
  4. Note the roll length at your roll width — that is the warehouse cut to request.
  5. Confirm the installer's measurement before the order is final.

Another worked example

Two bedrooms on one dye lot: 14 × 18 ft (252 sq ft) and 10 × 12 ft (120) total 372 sq ft; with 10% waste that is 409.2 sq ft = 45.47 sq yd, ordered as 46, cut 34.1 ft off the 12-ft roll — one continuous piece the installer seams on site.

Frequently asked questions

How much carpet do I need for a 12 × 15 ft room?

180 sq ft, which with a 10% waste allowance becomes 198 sq ft — exactly 22 square yards, and a 16.5-ft cut from a standard 12-ft roll. Padding matches at 22 sq yd.

Why is carpet sold by the square yard?

Historical convention from the broadloom trade — and it persists because carpet is manufactured in wide rolls. To convert: square yards = square feet ÷ 9. Retailers increasingly quote per sq ft; divide their per-yard price by 9 to compare honestly.

How much waste should I allow for carpet?

Ten percent for plain carpet in rectangular rooms. Fifteen to twenty percent for patterns (the repeat has to match at seams), and expect the installer to confirm — a room slightly wider than the roll forces a seam and a full extra width of material.

Does padding need the same amount?

Yes — padding covers every square foot the carpet does, and it is also rated in square yards. The estimator sizes both from the same with-waste area, so one number orders both.

Two rooms: one order or two?

One order from the same dye lot if the rooms share carpet — the estimator simply adds the areas. Running the rooms separately is right when they get different carpet or the install happens in phases.

🪵 Hard floors instead?

Does carpet come in 15-ft rolls too?

Yes — some mills offer 13.5-ft and 15-ft widths, which can eliminate a seam in a wide room and save more than the width premium costs. The estimator's roll-width field runs the length for any of the three.

Is padding thickness or density what matters?

Density. A ⅜-in, 8-lb density pad outlasts a plush ½-in, 4-lb pad under the same carpet, and carpet warranties often specify minimum density. Buy padding by the same square-yard count the estimator gives, at the density the carpet maker names.
