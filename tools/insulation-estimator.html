Insulation Estimator

Insulation is sold in packages and bags, not square feet. Tell this estimator the area and the R-value you are targeting, and it converts both into a shopping list — batts by the package, or blown-in by the bag.

For an attic, the area is the ceiling footprint below it. For walls, measure the wall area and subtract windows and doors — or use the gross area and let the offcuts fill gaps, which is what most installers do.

How it works

For batts and rolls, the estimator divides your area by the coverage printed on a typical package for that R-value — R-19 batts cover about 95 sq ft per package, R-38 about 60 — and rounds up, because partial packages are not sold. For blown-in, manufacturers rate bags per 1,000 sq ft at a given R-value and settled depth: about 22 bags per 1,000 sq ft for R-19, 40 for R-38, 52 for R-49 with typical fiberglass/cellulose products. R-value is resistance to heat flow per the whole assembly — doubling batts does not quite double performance if the cavity is compressed, which is why the coverage figures assume the insulation sits at its rated thickness, uncompressed.

Estimates only, from typical manufacturer coverage charts — always check the coverage printed on the product you actually buy. Blown-in counts assume the rated settled density; settling over the first year is normal and is built into the manufacturers' R-value charts.

Method & worked example

Method: Batts: packages = area ÷ the package coverage for the R-value (110 sq ft at R-13 down to 45 at R-49 on typical manufacturer charts). Blown-in: bags = area ÷ 1,000 × the bags-per-1,000 rate for the R-value (16 at R-13 up to 52 at R-49) at rated settled density. Both round up — partial packages are not sold.

Worked example: A 500 sq ft space insulated to R-19 with batts: 500 ÷ 95 sq ft per package = 6 packages, covering 570 sq ft. The same 500 sq ft blown in to R-38 instead would take 20 bags at 40 bags per 1,000 sq ft.

When to use this

  • Topping up an attic and deciding between a blower rental weekend or batt packages.
  • Insulating a new framed wall, floor or cathedral ceiling to a target R-value.
  • A utility rebate requires a stated R-value — you want the package count that reaches it.
  • Comparing blown-in vs batts on material count for the same space.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Measure the area: attic = ceiling footprint; walls = length × height minus openings.
  2. Pick the type: batts for open framed cavities, blown-in for attics and closed walls.
  3. Set the target R-value from your climate guidance or energy code.
  4. Buy the packages or bags shown — and one extra if a return trip is costly; unopened packages return easily.
  5. For blown-in, confirm the machine rental includes enough hose to reach the far corners.

Another worked example

An 800 sq ft attic floor brought to R-30 with blown-in instead: at 32 bags per 1,000 sq ft, that is 26 bags — a one-day blower rental job. The same space in R-30 batts would take 11 packages (800 ÷ 75), manageable without a machine but slower around every joist and wire.

Frequently asked questions

How much insulation do I need for a 500 sq ft attic at R-38?

Blown in, about 20 bags (40 bags per 1,000 sq ft × 0.5). In batts at R-38, packages cover roughly 60 sq ft each, so 500 sq ft takes 9 packages. The estimator above runs either version from the same two inputs.

What R-value should my attic have?

Most US climate guidance lands at R-38 to R-60 for attics, R-13 to R-21 for walls, and R-25 to R-30 for floors over unheated space. Cold northern zones sit at the top of each range. Your local energy code has the binding numbers for new work.

Batts or blown-in — which is better?

Blown-in fills odd corners and buries joists, so it usually wins on attics and retrofit work. Batts are cleaner for open wall cavities during framing and need no machine. Performance per R is the same; coverage quality is the difference.

How many bags of blown insulation cover 1,000 sq ft?

At R-38, about 40 bags of a typical product; at R-49, about 52; at R-19, about 22. Every manufacturer prints a coverage chart on the bag — bags per 1,000 sq ft at each R-value — and those numbers govern.

Can I add insulation over old insulation?

Usually yes — new batts (unfaced) or blown-in over existing attic insulation is standard practice, and the R-values simply add. Do not stack a second faced batt over a faced one; two vapor barriers can trap moisture.

How thick is R-38 in blown insulation?

About 10–11 in of settled blown fiberglass, or 13–14 in of cellulose, depending on product — the bag chart prints the depth for each R-value, and installers use depth rulers stapled to the joists to prove it. Buy by R-value and bag count, not by inches in the truck.

Should I air-seal before insulating?

Yes — the cheapest R-value is the air you stop moving. Sealing top-plate gaps, can-light boxes and plumbing penetrations before adding insulation routinely outperforms adding another R-10 on top of a leaky ceiling.
