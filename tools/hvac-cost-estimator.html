HVAC Cost Estimator

HVAC quotes are impossible to judge without the two variables that set them: the system type and the tonnage. Pick both — get the installed replacement range, scaled honestly, with a link to size the tonnage first if you do not know it.

Do not know your tonnage? The BTU Estimator sizes it from floor area, climate and insulation in under a minute — then come back with the number. Replacement assumes existing ductwork in serviceable condition.

How it works

Installed replacement bands at the reference 3-ton size: central AC $5,200–$8,500; furnace $3,800–$6,500; AC + furnace $8,500–$14,000; heat pump $6,500–$11,000; mini-split $3,800–$7,500 (single-zone). Size scales the band with the factor 0.70 + 0.10 × tons — a 2-ton system prices at 0.9× the 3-ton band, a 5-ton at 1.2× — because labour, ductwork and the truck roll do not shrink with the nameplate. High-efficiency tiers (SEER2 18+, variable speed) add 15–40% over these standard-efficiency bands and earn part of it back in bills.

Estimates only, typical US ranges for like-for-like replacement on existing ducts. New ductwork ($3,000–$6,000+), zoning, IAQ add-ons and refrigerant-line replacement are separate lines. Two quotes at the same tonnage can differ honestly on efficiency tier — compare SEER2 and warranty alongside price.

Method & worked example

Method: Installed range = 3-ton reference band by system (central AC $5,200–8,500; furnace $3,800–6,500; AC + furnace $8,500–14,000; heat pump $6,500–11,000; mini-split $3,800–7,500) × size factor (0.70 + 0.10 × tons), like-for-like on existing ducts.

Worked example: A 3-ton AC + furnace replacement: factor 1.00, so $8,500–$14,000 installed, mid-band $11,250.

When to use this

  • Your AC or furnace died and quotes are arriving under pressure.
  • You are planning a replacement before the failure forces the schedule.
  • A quote mixes system types and you need same-type comparisons.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Identify the system type being replaced.
  2. Get the tonnage — nameplate on the old unit, or the BTU Estimator.
  3. Read the installed range for your size factor.
  4. Compare quotes on efficiency tier and warranty alongside the band.

Another worked example

A 2-ton heat pump replacement: size factor 0.70 + 0.10 × 2 = 0.90, so the 3-ton reference band $6,500–$11,000 scales to $5,850–$9,900 installed, mid-band $7,875. In a mild climate that one box replaces both the AC and the furnace line — compare it against the combo's $8,500–$14,000 at 3 tons before deciding.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a new 3-ton AC and furnace cost?

$8,500–$14,000 installed, mid-band about $11,250, at standard efficiency with existing ducts. It is the most common full-system replacement in US housing, and the band most quotes should land inside.

How much is a 2-ton heat pump?

$5,850–$9,900 installed (the size factor at 2 tons is 0.9× the 3-ton reference band). Heat pumps price between AC-only and full combo systems, and in mild climates they replace both — heating included, no furnace required.

Repair or replace?

The trade's rule of thumb: multiply the repair quote by the system's age in years; if it exceeds the cost of a new system's value benchmark (often quoted against ~$5,000–$6,000), replace. A $900 repair on a 14-year-old system is $12,600 by that math — replace. The same repair at age 6 argues the other way.

Why does tonnage change price less than expected?

Because most of the invoice is not the box: labour, refrigerant, pads, disconnects, permits and duct transitions are near-fixed. Doubling tonnage adds perhaps 20% to the total — which is also why oversizing is a comfort mistake, not a budget catastrophe.

Are mini-splits cheaper?

Per zone, yes at the band above; per whole house with 4–5 zones, they meet or pass central pricing. Their case is zoning, no duct losses, and no ductwork requirement — price the configuration you actually need.

🌡️ Size the tonnage first?

Guessing tons is how systems get oversized. The BTU Estimator computes heating and cooling loads from your floor area, climate zone and insulation — then bring the tonnage back here.

What SEER rating should I buy?

The legal minimum (SEER2 ~13.4–14.3 by region) is the band basis; each step up costs more and saves a few percent on cooling bills. In hot climates the higher tiers pay back; in mild ones, spend on duct sealing first.

Should I replace AC and furnace together?

If both are near end-of-life, yes — matched systems share one labour mobilisation and warranty clock. If the furnace is young, AC-only replacement at its band is the honest buy.
