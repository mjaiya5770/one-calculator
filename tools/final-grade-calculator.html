How it works

Your course grade is a weighted average: current grade × (1 − final weight) + final score × final weight. The points from coursework are already banked — an 85% average with a 30% final means 59.5 points are locked in before you sit the exam. Solving the same equation backwards gives the needed score: (target − banked points) ÷ final weight. If that number is over 100, the target is out of reach even with a perfect paper; under 0, the grade is already secured.

Grading scales vary by school and instructor (curves, 10-point vs 7-point scales, dropped scores). Treat this as the straight math on the syllabus weights you enter.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Needed final score = (target grade − current grade × (1 − final weight)) ÷ final weight. Your banked points are current grade × (1 − final weight).

Worked example: With a current grade of 85%, a final worth 30% and a target of 88%: you have 59.5 points banked, so (88 − 59.5) ÷ 0.30 = 95 — you need a 95 on the final.

When to use this

  • The syllabus says the final is worth a big share of the grade and you want the exact score an A, B or C requires.
  • You are deciding where tonight's study hours go and one course needs a near-perfect paper while another is already safe.
  • You scored the final in your head on the way out and want to turn a guessed score into the actual course grade.
  • A target grade decides a scholarship, a prerequisite or academic standing and guessing is not good enough.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Choose the mode first: What do I need? for a target, or What if I get…? for a score you have in mind.
  2. Enter your current grade as a percent — the average on everything except the final.
  3. Enter the final's weight from the syllabus, as a percent of the whole course grade.
  4. In needed mode, set your target course grade; the headline gives the required final score and the table repeats it for the A, B, C and D ranges.
  5. In what-if mode, enter a final score and read the course grade and letter it produces, then try a score ten points either side to see how much room you have.

Another worked example

Using needed mode: a current grade of 78%, a final worth 40% and a target of 85%. Your coursework has already banked 46.8 points, so the calculator says you need 95.5% on the final to finish at 85% — possible, but it takes a near-perfect paper. Even a 100% final tops out at an 86.8% course grade, which is why the A range at 90% is not on the table in this class.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find out what my final is worth?

It is in the course syllabus, usually as a percentage of the total grade ("Final exam: 30%"). If the syllabus lists points instead, divide the final's points by the total course points — 150 of 500 points is a 30% weight.

What if the calculator says I need more than 100%?

Then that target grade is mathematically out of reach — even a perfect final cannot pull the average high enough. Look at the next letter down in the table; it is often still very achievable, and knowing that before the exam beats finding out after.

My needed score is negative. What does that mean?

Your target is already secured: your banked coursework points alone reach it, so any final score — even a zero — keeps that grade. (Still show up: many courses fail students who skip the final regardless of points.)

Does this work with letter grades instead of percentages?

Convert with your school's scale first (A ≈ 90–100, B ≈ 80–89 on a 10-point scale), or use the GPA Calculator for a whole semester of letter grades and credits.

What if I still have homework or another test besides the final?

This tool treats the final as the only grade still to come, so fold everything else into the current grade first: update your average once the last homework or quiz is graded, then run the final's weight against that figure. If two big assessments remain, run the tool twice — once treating the nearer one as the 'final' to set a target for it, then again for the real final.

Does a curve change the score I need?

The math here is the uncurved version: it uses the weights and percents you enter, exactly as printed in the syllabus. A curve applied afterwards can only move your course grade up from the figure shown, so treat the needed score as the no-curve requirement — the safest number to study toward.
