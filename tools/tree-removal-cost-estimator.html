Tree Removal Cost Estimator

Tree quotes swing from a few hundred to several thousand for reasons that are mostly visible from the ground. This estimator prices them openly: published height bands, an access multiplier, stump grinding by diameter — the same structure the pros quote from.

The height bands are built from published US cost guides fetched Nov 2026 (HomeAdvisor puts the average removal at $750 with a normal range of $200–$2,000; HomeGuide, Angi and Airtasker US publish matching height bands). They are planning ranges, printed as such — a licensed arborist’s on-site quote is the only firm price, and dead, leaning or line-adjacent trees belong to professionals, full stop.

How it works

Start with the height band: small (under 30 ft) $150–$500, medium (30–60 ft) $450–$1,200, large (60–80 ft) $800–$1,700, extra large (80 ft+) $1,000–$2,500. Access adjusts the band: open ×1.0, tight ×1.25 (rigging and piecing down instead of felling), hazard ×1.5 (over structures, lines, or a dead/leaning trunk). Stump grinding prices by diameter at $2–$5 per inch with a $100 minimum and a band at least $50 wide — a 24 in stump prices at $100–$150. Haul-away adds $70–$200; a crane day adds $250–$600. Multiple trees multiply the per-tree figure, though real crews usually discount the second tree’s mobilization.

Estimates from published guide ranges (Nov 2026), not quotes. Species and condition matter — a rotten trunk can go either way (easier to fell, more dangerous to climb). Anything within striking distance of a power line is the utility’s or a line-clearance arborist’s job, and many cities require a permit for street or heritage trees.

Method & worked example

Method: Cost = height band × access multiplier + stump + haul-away + crane, per tree. Bands (published US guide consensus, Nov 2026): small $150–$500, medium $450–$1,200, large $800–$1,700, extra large $1,000–$2,500. Access: open ×1.0, tight ×1.25, hazard ×1.5. Stump grinding $2–$5 per inch of diameter, $100 minimum, band at least $50 wide. Haul-away $70–$200; crane $250–$600.

Worked example: A medium tree, open access, 18 in stump ground: $450–$1,200 + $100–$150 stump = $550–$1,350 (midpoint $950).

When to use this

  • A tree service quote landed and you want a sanity check before signing.
  • You are budgeting a yard cleanup with several removals.
  • A dead or leaning tree needs a planning number for insurance or a sale.
  • You are deciding between grinding the stump and leaving it.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Estimate the height honestly — a two-story house is about 25 ft at the eaves; most mature shade trees are 40–60 ft.
  2. Pick the access rating by what is underneath and beside the tree, not by how the yard feels.
  3. Measure the stump (or trunk) diameter at its widest, flare included.
  4. Add haul-away or crane only if they truly apply.
  5. Read the range and the midpoint; get two on-site quotes for anything in the hazard class.

Another worked example

Two large trees (60–80 ft) rated hazard, 24 in stumps ground, debris hauled: per tree ($800–$1,700 × 1.5) + stump $100–$150 + haul $70–$200 = $1,370–$2,900; two trees $2,740–$5,800, midpoint $4,270. This is the class of job where the second quote matters most — rigging-heavy work is where bids diverge.

Frequently asked questions

What does it cost to remove a medium tree?

A 30–60 ft tree in the open with an 18 in stump ground: $550–$1,350, midpoint about $950. That matches the published guides’ center of gravity — HomeAdvisor’s national average removal is $750 before stump work. Tight access or a hazard rating pushes the same tree toward $1,500+.

Why does access change the price so much?

An open tree can be felled in one or two cuts. A tree over your roof has to be climbed and lowered piece by piece on ropes — hours of skilled labor instead of minutes, with real risk priced in. The multipliers here (×1.25 tight, ×1.5 hazard) are the planning-level version of that difference.

How is stump grinding priced?

By diameter, because grinding time scales with the cross-section: guides put it at $2–$5 per inch of diameter, typically with a minimum around $100–$150 per stump, and full removal of the root ball costs far more. An 18 in stump prices at $100–$150 here. Measuring across the widest point, flare included, is the honest diameter.

Is it cheaper to remove several trees at once?

Usually, yes — the crew, truck and chipper are already there. This estimator multiplies the full per-tree range, so treat its multi-tree total as the ceiling: two large hazard trees with haul-away price at $2,740–$5,800 here, and a real crew bidding both on one visit commonly lands under the top of that range. Get it quoted as one job, not two.

Can I save money by keeping the wood?

Keeping the wood skips haul-away ($70–$200) and some crews discount further if you take the brush too. Firewood value is real only if the species burns well and you will split and season it — a cord of green oak is a winter of work, not a windfall. Never keep wood from a diseased tree near healthy ones of the same species.

When is a crane actually needed?

When there is no drop zone at all — a tree boxed in by structures, a pool and lines, or a dead trunk too unsafe to climb. The crane lifts sections out whole. It adds $250–$600 at planning level here, and on genuinely boxed-in trees it can be the cheaper option versus a full day of rigging.

Do I need a permit to remove my own tree?

Sometimes. Many cities protect street trees, heritage trees above a trunk diameter, and trees in setback or conservation zones — and HOAs add their own rules. The permit is usually cheap; the fine for skipping it is not. Check before the crew arrives, not after.
