Topsoil Estimator

Topsoil is ordered three ways — by the yard, by the ton, by the bag — and the conversions are where orders go wrong. Enter area and depth once; get all three, with the settle factor gardeners actually see.

Rules of depth: 2 in refreshes a tired bed, 4 in builds a new one, 6 in is the minimum for a raised bed you actually plant in, and new lawns want 4–6 in under the seed or sod.

How it works

Volume is area × depth: cubic feet = length × width × (depth ÷ 12), and cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27. Weight uses the standard topsoil planning density of about 1.1 tons per cubic yard (roughly 2,200 lb — a screened, reasonably dry topsoil; wet soil can run 20% heavier, which matters for your truck, not your wallet, since bulk is sold by the yard). Bag counts divide the cubic feet by the bag size. Depths are finished depths: loose soil settles and compacts about 15–20% in the first season, so for an exact finished depth, order a fifth extra — the estimator reports the true geometric volume so you can see the choice.

Estimates only. Bulk topsoil quality varies enormously — ask whether it is screened, and what the soil is sourced from. Delivery is usually economical from 2–3 yards up; below that, bags avoid the delivery fee.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Cubic feet = length × width × depth in feet; cubic yards = cu ft ÷ 27. Tons = yards × 1.1 (the standard planning density for screened topsoil, ≈2,200 lb per yard). Bags = cu ft ÷ bag size in cu ft.

Worked example: A 20 × 10 ft bed at 4 in deep: 66.67 cu ft = 2.47 cu yd ≈ 2.72 tons — or 45 bags at 1.5 cu ft. At 6 in deep the same bed needs 3.70 yards, which is where bulk delivery firmly beats bags.

When to use this

  • Building raised beds and choosing between a bulk yard delivery and bags.
  • Top-dressing or regrading a lawn area before seeding or sod.
  • Filling a garden bed, berm or tree ring to a stated depth.
  • Pricing a delivery: yards for the yard, tons for the ticket, bags for the store run.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Measure the area's length and width (break irregular beds into rectangles).
  2. Set the depth: 2 in refresh, 4 in new bed, 6 in raised bed minimum.
  3. Read yards for bulk, tons for weight limits, bags for small jobs.
  4. Add a fifth extra if an exact finished depth matters after settling.
  5. Schedule delivery after the area is cleared and edged — soil waits poorly in a driveway pile.

A bigger kitchen-garden build: 30 × 15 ft at 6 in deep. That is 225 cu ft = 8.33 cu yd, about 9.17 tons — firmly bulk-delivery territory, since the bag equivalent is 113 of the 2 cu ft bags, a pallet-plus of lifting.

Frequently asked questions

How much topsoil do I need for a 20 × 10 ft bed at 4 inches?

200 sq ft × 4 in = 66.67 cu ft = 2.47 cubic yards — about 2.72 tons, or 45 bags at 1.5 cu ft each. That is the size where bulk delivery usually beats bagging it.

How much does a cubic yard of topsoil weigh?

About 2,200 lb (1.1 tons) for typical screened topsoil at average moisture. Saturated soil can reach 2,700 lb per yard. A half-ton pickup carries roughly half a yard safely — plan trips accordingly.

How deep should topsoil be for a new lawn?

Four to six inches over the graded subsoil. Less and roots hit the poor layer before they establish; the estimator at 6 in over 200 sq ft calls for 3.70 yards. Raised beds want 6 in minimum, 12 in for root vegetables.

Bags or bulk — where is the break-even?

Bags run roughly 3–5× the per-yard price of bulk. Under about half a yard, bags win on total cost because bulk carries a delivery fee. The bag count is always shown here so you can price both honestly.

Why does my delivered yard look short after a month?

Settling. Loose-placed topsoil compacts 15–20% as it wets and settles. Order a fifth extra for a precise finished grade, or accept the gentle dip as the bed's first-season character.

How many bags of topsoil make a cubic yard?

Twenty-seven 1 cu ft bags, eighteen 1.5 cu ft bags, or 13.5 of the 2 cu ft size. The estimator's bag count is why bulk wins past about half a yard: at yard 1 you are choosing between a delivery and roughly 18–27 bag trips to the car.

Topsoil or garden soil — are they the same?

No. Topsoil is screened earth — structure and bulk. Bagged 'garden soil' is topsoil amended with compost and fertiliser for beds. For lawns and grading use topsoil; for small planted beds, garden soil earns its premium in the first season.
