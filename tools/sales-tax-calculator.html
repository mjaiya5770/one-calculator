How it works

Sales tax is price × tax rate, added to the sticker price. The rate you pay is set by your state plus any county and city add-ons — which is why two towns in the same state can charge different totals.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Tax = price × tax rate. Total = price × (1 + tax rate). The reverse mode divides a tax-inclusive total by (1 + rate) to recover the pre-tax price.

Worked example: A $120 purchase at a 7.25% rate adds $8.70 of tax, for a total of $128.70.

When to use this

  • You are budgeting a larger purchase — a laptop, appliance or car part — and the sticker price is not what will leave your account.
  • You are a seller setting a tax-inclusive shelf price and need to know how much of a given sticker figure is tax at your local rate.
  • You moved or are shopping across a county line and want to see what a different combined rate does to the same basket.
  • A receipt total looks off and you want to check the tax line against the price and rate yourself.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Enter the pre-tax price of the item or basket.
  2. Enter your combined sales tax rate — state base plus county and city add-ons where you are buying.
  3. Click Calculate tax and read the tax amount and the total with tax together.
  4. If you shop in more than one town, re-run at the other town's rate; the difference on a big purchase can be worth the drive, or not.

Another worked example

In add mode (price first, tax on top): a $249.50 purchase at an 8.25% combined rate adds $20.58 of sales tax, for a total of $270.08 out the door. The tax alone is more than many people guess on a mid-size purchase, which is why checking it before the till beats being surprised at it.

Frequently asked questions

What does sales tax apply to?

Usually tangible goods and some services, but rules vary by state. Groceries, clothing, and digital goods are taxed in some states and exempt in others — check your state's revenue site for specifics.

Why do tax rates differ so much?

States set a base rate, then counties and cities can add their own. That's why the hint above shows state bases — your actual checkout rate may be higher once local taxes are included.

Is sales tax deductible?

Sometimes. Itemizers can deduct state and local sales tax instead of state income tax on federal returns (the SALT deduction, subject to caps). Keep receipts if you make big purchases — this is general info, not tax advice.

How do I get the pre-tax price back from a tax-inclusive total?

Divide the total by 1 plus the rate as a decimal. A $270.08 total at 8.25% divides by 1.0825 to give the $249.50 pre-tax price, and the remainder, $20.58, was the tax. This calculator itself works in the forward direction — price and rate in, tax and total out — so use that division by hand when a receipt only shows the combined figure.

Is sales tax charged on shipping and delivery fees?

It depends on the state and on how the charge is presented. Many states tax delivery when it is part of a taxable sale and the fee is not separately stated, and exempt it when it is separately stated or the goods themselves are exempt. There is no single national rule, so check your state revenue site for a large shipped purchase.

Which rate applies when I buy online from another state?

Usually the rate where the goods are delivered, not where the seller sits — most states use destination sourcing, and large online sellers collect that destination tax at checkout. That is why the same cart can be taxed differently after you change the delivery address.
