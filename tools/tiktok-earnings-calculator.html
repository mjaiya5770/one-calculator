TikTok Earnings Calculator

TikTok pays for views through its creator rewards program, but the per-view rate is unpublished, region-dependent and only counts qualified views. This calculator works in honest ranges, applies the qualified-views share most calculators skip, and shows why brand deals are the real money.

These are editorial planning ranges, labeled as estimates: TikTok does not publish a rate card, per-view rewards vary by country, video length and watch quality, and only "qualified" views — real views of eligible videos over one minute, from the For You feed — earn. If your videos are under a minute or much of your audience is outside program countries, lower the qualified share. TikTok Shop commissions, LIVE gifts and brand deals are separate income streams, not included here.

How it works

Monthly rewards = views × qualified share ÷ 1,000 × the per-1,000 rate. The qualified share is the step most calculators skip: views from search, profiles, duets or very short videos may not earn at all, and a video under the program's length threshold earns nothing no matter how far it travels. The range exists because the true rate floats with region and engagement quality — a US audience watching full one-minute videos sits near the top of reported ranges, a fast-skipping international audience near the bottom.

Planning estimates only — not a promise of income and not TikTok-published figures. Program names, eligibility and rates change frequently and differ by country; your own TikTok Studio analytics show what your account actually earned per 1,000 qualified views, which is the only rate that describes your channel.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Monthly rewards = monthly views × qualified-views share ÷ 1,000 × rate per 1,000 qualified views. Only qualified views — genuine For You views of eligible videos at least one minute long — earn anything.

Worked example: 500,000 views at 80% qualified = 400,000 qualified views; at the $0.70 mid rate: 400 × $0.70 = $280 a month.

When to use this

  • You are projecting whether TikTok can be a real income line or is still a hobby
  • A month went viral and you want a sane estimate of what it actually earned
  • You are comparing TikTok against YouTube for the same content effort
  • You are setting a savings or tax-withholding target on creator income

How to use it, step by step

  1. Enter your typical monthly views — a normal month, not the viral one.
  2. Estimate your qualified share honestly: over a minute long, For You driven, program countries.
  3. Pick the reported range, or the conservative one if your audience is global.
  4. If you have payout history, enter your real rate as a custom mid.
  5. Read the low/mid/high as a planning band, then price brand deals separately — they are the real income.
  6. Recheck monthly: your own per-1,000 rate from TikTok Studio replaces every estimate here.

Another worked example

A bigger account with softer numbers: 2,000,000 views a month, but only 60% qualified (short clips and search traffic mixed in) on the conservative $0.35 mid rate: 1,200,000 qualified views × $0.35 = $420 a month — a range of $240 to $600. Ten times the views of the worked example on the formula card pays only 1.5× the money, because qualified share and rate matter as much as raw views.

Frequently asked questions

How much does TikTok pay for 1 million views?

On the reported range, 1,000,000 views with 80% qualified at a $0.70 mid rate works out to about $560. At the low end ($0.40, fewer qualified views) it can be under $300; the older, much smaller per-view fund payouts people quote from 2021 are not comparable to the current rewards program.

Why does the qualified-views share matter so much?

Only qualified views earn: they must be genuine views of an eligible video (over a minute long) from the For You feed, in a program country. A viral video driven by search traffic or shares can have a third of its views not count. Setting the share honestly turns a fantasy number into a planning number.

Do short TikTok videos earn view rewards?

Under the current creator rewards structure, videos must be at least one minute long to earn view rewards. Shorter videos still build audience and can carry TikTok Shop links or brand deals — they just do not earn from the view-reward pool.

What actually pays most TikTok creators?

Brand deals and TikTok Shop commissions, by a wide margin for most accounts. A single sponsored video priced with the Sponsorship Rate Calculator commonly pays what hundreds of thousands of rewarded views pay. Treat view rewards as a floor, not the business model.

Why do two creators report such different per-view rates?

Region mix is the biggest factor — advertisers pay more for US, UK and EU audiences — followed by video length and completion rate. The program rewards watch quality, so a 60-second video watched to the end earns more per view than one skipped at five seconds.

Why did my per-view rate drop as I grew?

Viral reach is usually international and fast-skipping — lower qualified share and lower watch quality per view. Steady niche content in program countries often earns a better rate on fewer views. The calculator's qualified-share input exists to make this visible instead of mysterious.

Are TikTok Shop earnings included?

No — Shop commissions are a product margin, not a view reward, and they dwarf view rewards for many sellers. This calculator prices the view-reward stream only; add your Shop and LIVE income separately when you total the business.
