How it works

With monthly rate r over n months, the required deposit is PMT = (FV − PV×(1+r)n) × r / ((1+r)n − 1). Your starting balance grows with compound interest, and each deposit compounds from the month it's made — so earlier months work harder than later ones.

Assumes deposits at the end of each month and a constant rate. Real account rates fluctuate.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Required monthly deposit PMT = (FV − PV × (1+r)n) × r ÷ ((1+r)n − 1), where FV is the goal, PV what you already have, r the monthly rate and n the months.

Worked example: To reach $20,000 in 3 years with $2,000 already saved and a 5% return, you need to deposit $456.14/month.

When to use this

  • A named goal — deposit, car, trip, emergency fund — needs a monthly number and a date.
  • You have a deadline and a starting amount and want the required monthly saving.
  • You are choosing between two goals and can only fund one properly this year.
  • You want to see what your savings account's interest quietly contributes.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Enter the goal amount.
  2. Add what you have already saved toward it.
  3. Set the months until the deadline.
  4. Add the account's interest rate if it earns one.
  5. Read the monthly amount — then set the transfer for the day after payday.

Another worked example

Goal $12,000 in 18 months, starting from $2,000 in a 4% account: the starting money grows to $2,123.46 on its own, and you need to add $533.31 a month to land exactly on target. Without interest the figure would be $555.56 — the account quietly covers about $400 of the goal.

Frequently asked questions

Why does starting early matter so much?

Compound interest rewards time: at 4%, starting with $1,000 and saving for 12 months earns you free money from interest — the longer the timeline, the more the account pays you to save.

Where should I keep goal savings?

A high-yield savings account (typically 4–5% APY as of 2026) beats a checking account and keeps the money liquid and FDIC-insured. Try 0.5% vs 4.5% above to see the difference.

What if I fall behind?

Recalculate with your new starting balance and remaining months — the monthly number will rise, but catching up now beats starting over later.

Should I count investment returns for a short goal?

For goals under about two years, use a savings-account rate, not stock-market returns — a dip at the wrong month breaks deadlines. This tool's rate field is built for deposit interest; long goals belong in the Retirement or Compound Interest tools.
