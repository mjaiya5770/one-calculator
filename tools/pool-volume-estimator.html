Pool Volume Estimator

Every chemical dose, heater size and filter schedule starts with one number: how much water is in the pool. Pick the shape, enter the dimensions and the shallow/deep depths — get gallons.

Average depth for a sloped-bottom pool is (shallow + deep) ÷ 2 — a 3-ft shallow and 6-ft deep end average 4.5 ft. Constant-depth and above-ground pools just use their wall depth minus a few inches of freeboard.

Tip: dimensions accept tape-measure entries — try 6' 8 5/16" or 6.5.

How it works

Rectangles are length × width × average depth. Circles use π × radius2 × depth; ovals use length × width × π ÷ 4 × depth (the ellipse area). Cubic feet convert to US gallons at × 7.48052. The average-depth convention — (shallow + deep) ÷ 2 — assumes a steady slope; pools with a sharp deep-end drop-off hold a little less than the formula says, and beach entries hold less still, which is why chemical dosing always ends with a water test rather than blind faith in any volume figure.

Estimates only. For chemical dosing, round to the nearest 500 gallons and confirm with test strips after treatment. Kidney and freeform pools: use the oval setting with the longest length and widest width, then shave about 10% off the result.

Formula & worked example

Formula: Rectangular: L × W × average depth. Round: π × (diameter ÷ 2)² × depth. Oval: L × W × π ÷ 4 × depth. Gallons = cubic feet × 7.48052. Average depth = (shallow end + deep end) ÷ 2.

Worked example: A 30 × 15 ft pool averaging 4.5 ft deep: 2,025 cu ft × 7.48052 = 15,148 gallons. An 18-ft round pool at 4 ft averages 7,614 gallons — and weighs about 32 tons of water, which is why above-ground pools want level, compacted ground.

When to use this

  • Dosing chlorine, shock or algaecide — every label is written per 10,000 gallons.
  • Sizing a pump, filter or heater replacement to the water it must turn over.
  • Filling a new pool and predicting the water bill (or the truck loads).
  • Comparing salt-system cell sizes, which are rated by pool gallons.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Pick the shape closest to your pool.
  2. Measure waterline dimensions, not coping: length and width (diameter if round).
  3. Set average depth: (shallow + deep) ÷ 2 for sloped bottoms.
  4. Use the gallon figure for dosing; round to the nearest 500 as labels assume.
  5. Re-check after any depth-changing renovation — a resurfaced deep end changes the volume.

Another worked example

An oval pool, 24 × 12 ft at a steady 4 ft average depth (a typical above-ground): the ellipse factor gives 904.8 cu ft — 6,768 gallons. Dosed as a 7,000-gallon pool it behaves; dosed as its 30-ft rectangular cousin it would be chronically over-chlorinated.

Frequently asked questions

How many gallons is a 30 × 15 ft pool?

At a 4.5-ft average depth: 30 × 15 × 4.5 = 2,025 cu ft × 7.48052 = 15,148 gallons. That is the mid-size residential pool most chemical charts are silently written for.

How do I find my pool's average depth?

Add the shallow-end and deep-end depths and halve them. A pool that runs 3 ft to 8 ft averages 5.5 ft — not 8, which is the single most common volume mistake and leads to systematic chemical overdosing.

How many gallons does a round 18-ft pool hold?

π × 9² × 4 ft average depth = 1,018 cu ft ≈ 7,614 gallons. Round above-ground pools are usually shallower than they look — most are 4 ft of water in a 52-in wall.

Why does pool volume matter so much?

Chlorine, shock, algaecide, salt and acid are all dosed per 10,000 gallons. A 20% volume error is a 20% dosing error on everything, forever — it is the cheapest number in pool ownership to get right once.

How long should my pump run?

The rule of thumb is one full turnover per day in season. A 15,148-gallon pool with a 40-gallon-per-minute pump turns over in about 6.3 hours — the volume from this estimator divided by your pump's gallons per minute gives your runtime directly.

How many gallons is a 24-ft round above-ground pool?

At 4 ft of water: π × 12² × 4 = 1,810 cu ft ≈ 13,536 gallons. Most '24-ft' pools hold closer to 13,500 than the 15,000+ often quoted from wall height rather than water depth.

Does the volume include the deep-end hopper shape?

The (shallow + deep) ÷ 2 average assumes a steady slope. A hopper bottom (flat deep floor with sloped sides) holds slightly less — roughly 5% under the estimate. For dosing, that error is inside the label's own rounding; for a water-truck order, mention the shape.
