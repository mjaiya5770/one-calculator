House Cleaning Cost Estimator

Cleaning quotes look arbitrary until you see the model: square feet × a service rate, plus bedrooms, times a frequency discount. This estimator shows that model — standard, deep and move-out priced separately, recurring discounts applied openly.

First visits are almost always priced as deep cleans even on a recurring plan — the discount applies once the home is on a maintenance rhythm. Tipping, specialty requests (inside oven/fridge, windows) and pet-heavy homes adjust real quotes around these bands.

How it works

The industry model: a per-square-foot rate by service type — standard $0.09, deep $0.16, move-out $0.19 per sq ft — plus about $10 per bedroom, then the frequency discount: monthly ×0.90, biweekly ×0.85, weekly ×0.80. The range shown is 0.85×–1.2× the point price, covering crew size, local wages and home condition. Crew time at the planning rate of $45/hr is shown too — a $192 clean is about 4.3 crew-hours, which is how you sanity-check a "two hours, $300" quote.

Estimates only, typical US ranges for insured services; independent cleaners often price 15–30% under agency rates. Homes with heavy clutter, biohazard or hoarding conditions are specialty work outside these bands entirely.

Method & worked example

Method: Per-visit price = (home sq ft × type rate — standard $0.09, deep $0.16, move-out $0.19 — + $10 per bedroom) × frequency factor (one-time 1.0, monthly 0.90, biweekly 0.85, weekly 0.80). Typical range 0.85×–1.2× the point price; crew time at $45/hr.

Worked example: An 1,800 sq ft, 3-bedroom standard one-time clean: base $192, price $192 per visit (range $163–$230), about 4.3 crew-hours.

When to use this

  • You are hiring a cleaning service and the first quote needs a benchmark.
  • You are choosing between one-time, monthly and biweekly plans in real dollars.
  • You are pricing a move-out clean against your deposit.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Enter the home's square footage and bedrooms.
  2. Pick the cleaning type — deep for first visits and move-outs.
  3. Pick the frequency to see its discount applied.
  4. Use the crew-hours line to judge per-hour quotes against per-visit quotes.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to clean an 1,800 sq ft house?

Standard one-time clean, 3 bedrooms: $192 per visit (range $163–$230), about 4.3 crew-hours. The same home on a biweekly plan prices at about $163 per visit — frequency is the cheapest discount in home services.

How much is a deep clean for a 1,200 sq ft home?

Two bedrooms, deep clean on a biweekly plan: $180.20 per visit (range $153–$216) — the deep rate ($0.16/sq ft) minus the 15% frequency discount. A one-time deep clean of the same home runs about $212.

Why does a deep clean cost nearly double?

It is a different job list: inside appliances, baseboards, vents, grout attention, wall spots — typically 1.5–2× the hours of a maintenance visit. Services require it first because recurring pricing assumes a deep-cleaned starting point.

Is weekly or biweekly better value?

Biweekly is the market's sweet spot — 15% off and the home never fully relapses. Weekly (20% off) suits large families and pet-heavy homes; monthly (10% off) is really a recurring deep-ish clean wearing a discount.

How do cleaners charge — per hour or per visit?

Both models exist. Per-visit pricing (this estimator's model) protects you from slow crews; per-hour protects the cleaner from underestimated homes. Convert with the crew-hours line: at $45/hr planning, a $192 quote should buy roughly 4+ hours of actual cleaning.

What is included in a standard clean?

Kitchens and bathrooms sanitised, floors vacuumed/mopped, dusting, mirrors, trash. Inside appliances, windows and laundry are add-ons at most services — confirm the checklist before comparing prices.

Should I tip the cleaners?

Customary at 10–20% for one-time and holiday cleans; recurring clients often tip per visit lightly or in a year-end amount. It is appreciated, rarely required, and never in these estimates.
