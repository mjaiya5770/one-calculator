Drywall Cost Estimator

The materials calculator counts sheets; this one prices the finished wall. Pick a finish level and get the installed cost range — materials, hang-and-finish labor and optional tear-out — in labeled 2026 planning ranges.

Rates are labeled planning ranges, not quotes: sheets at $13.00–$15.50 per 4×8 (Fixr drywall guide, Jan 2025), joint compound and supplies at $0.16/sq ft combined, labor by finish level from the same guide, tear-out at a $0.50/sq ft planning figure. Pros in high-cost metros land above the top of the range; small jobs carry minimums no per-foot math captures.

How it works

Wall area is perimeter × ceiling height, plus the ceiling if selected. Sheets = area × (1 + waste) ÷ sheet size, rounded up — and materials are priced on the sheets you actually buy, not the net area. Materials per square foot = sheet price ÷ 32 + $0.10 compound + $0.06 tape/screws/corner bead. Labor is the finish level’s published range per square foot of wall area: Level 0 $0.30–0.80, Level 3 $0.90–2.50, Level 4 $1.00–3.00, Level 5 $1.15–3.50. Tear-out, when selected, adds $0.50/sq ft.

Planning ranges only, labeled as estimates — your market, ceiling height, number of corners and openings, and whether the crew also primes will move a real quote. The widely quoted installed band for standard work is $1.50–$3.50 per sq ft all-in (HomeGuide, Sep 2025); use this tool to see which line items push you within it.

Method & worked example

Method: Installed cost = materials + labor + tear-out. Materials = sheets bought × (sheet price ÷ 32 + $0.16/sq ft for compound, tape, screws and bead), with sheets at $13.00–$15.50 per 4×8 (Fixr, Jan 2025). Labor = wall area × finish-level range (L0 $0.30–0.80, L3 $0.90–2.50, L4 $1.00–3.00, L5 $1.15–3.50 per sq ft). Tear-out adds $0.50/sq ft when selected. All figures are labeled 2026 planning ranges.

Worked example: A 12×10 room, 8 ft ceilings, walls + ceiling = 472 sq ft at Level 4: 17 sheets of 4×8, materials $308–$351, total installed $780–$1,767.

When to use this

  • You have a materials count and need the finished-wall budget number.
  • Two contractor quotes differ wildly and you want to see which line item drives it.
  • You are choosing between Level 4 and Level 5 under gloss paint or raking light.
  • A remodel needs tear-out priced in before you commit to the scope.

How to use it, step by step

  1. Enter room length, width and ceiling height; choose walls-only or walls + ceiling.
  2. Pick the sheet size your crew will hang (4×12 cuts joint-finishing labor).
  3. Select the finish level the paint job actually needs.
  4. Add tear-out if old board is coming down.
  5. Read the range: materials, labor band and total — then sanity-check against the $1.50–$3.50/sq ft installed band.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to drywall a 12×10 room?

Walls plus ceiling at 8 ft is 472 sq ft. At Level 4 with 4×8 sheets: 17 sheets, materials $308–$351, labor $472–$1,416 — a total of about $780–$1,767. The range is wide because labor, not board, is most of the bill, and labor rates vary more than any other input.

What does finish level actually mean?

Levels come from the Gypsum Association standard. Level 0 is hung board with no finishing. Level 3 is taped, one coat, made for heavy texture. Level 4 — taped, two coats on joints, one on fasteners — is the normal standard under flat paint. Level 5 adds a full skim coat and is specified under gloss paint or harsh side-lighting, where Level 4 joints can photograph through.

Is it cheaper to hang 4×12 sheets?

The board costs about the same per square foot, but a 20×14 room with 9 ft walls takes 15 sheets of 4×12 against 22 of 4×8 — fewer joints to tape and finish, which is where the money is. The trade-off is handling: 4×12 sheets need two people and stairwell clearance. Pros default to 4×12 on ceilings and long walls for exactly this reason.

What does tear-out add?

At the $0.50/sq ft planning figure, 612 sq ft of walls adds $306, plus disposal reality: a room of old drywall is roughly a pickup load or a small dumpster line item. Tear-out also exposes the framing — budget a contingency for the insulation, wiring or rot surprises that are the usual reason for tearing out in the first place.

Should I hang it myself and hire out the finishing?

That split is the classic DIY compromise and it prices well here: hanging is Level 0 labor ($0.30–$0.80/sq ft) while finishing is the skilled, visible part. Many finishers charge a premium to finish DIY hanging, or refuse it — confirm before you hang, and hang it to a finisher’s standard: tight joints, screws seated, no broken corners.

Why is my quote above the top of this range?

Common reasons: a job minimum on a small room, high ceilings or scaffold work, lots of outside corners and soffits, Level 5 specified under critical light, or a metro labor market. The range assumes straightforward rectangular rooms at standard height — count your complications honestly and expect the top of the range or beyond.

Does this include paint and primer?

No. Drywall finishing ends at a sanded, ready-to-prime surface. PVA primer plus two finish coats is a separate line — the Painting Cost Estimator prices it per room. Never skip the primer line in a budget: raw compound absorbs paint unevenly without it.
