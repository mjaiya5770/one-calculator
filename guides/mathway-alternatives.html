Mathway Alternatives: Free Step-by-Step Math Help

Looking for a Mathway alternative that shows the working for free? An honest comparison of free step-by-step math help — including our fraction and scientific calculators, which show every step at no cost.

Alternatives · Published November 8, 2026 · One Calculator

Searching for a Mathway alternative usually starts with one specific need: the steps. Mathway is a well-known problem solver covering a wide range of math, from basic arithmetic to calculus. Getting the final answer is free, but its step-by-step solutions are part of a paid subscription. For homework, the steps are the point. Students often go looking for an alternative because they need the working shown without paying for another subscription.

One Calculator is a calculator library, not a photo-a-problem solver, and it does not cover calculus problem-solving the way Mathway does. What it does offer is free step-by-step help on several common tools. This comparison looks at the specific reason many people search for an alternative: free step-by-step working on problems where the method matters as much as the answer.

One Calculator does not replace Mathway for broad typed problem solving or calculus. It replaces the paid step-by-step part for specific calculators that already show their work.

What you actually want when you search for an alternative

Most students are not looking for a different answer engine. They are looking for the working. A final answer tells you whether you got it right; the steps tell you why. If you cannot show the method, a correct answer may not help on homework, quizzes, or when you sit down to study a similar problem.

That is the real gap. A problem solver with broad coverage but paywalled steps is less useful for practice. You need to see the process enough times that you can reproduce it yourself. A free calculator that shows every step is often more useful for that job than a broader tool that stops at the answer.

Where Mathway is stronger

Mathway has real breadth. It covers a wide range of math, from basic arithmetic to calculus, and it works as a typed problem solver. If you need one engine that can move from algebra to trigonometry to calculus, that is where Mathway has an edge.

One Calculator does not try to match that. It is not a photo-a-problem solver, and it does not cover calculus problem-solving the way Mathway does. If you are in calculus and need a full worked solution from an expression or equation, Mathway’s subscription may still be the better fit.

Where the free route wins

One Calculator wins on a narrower set of tasks where the working is part of the answer. The tools are built to show what happened, not just return a result. You do not need an account, and there is no subscription for the steps.

These are not hidden teaser results. The working is shown directly in the tool, not unlocked after signup.

Fraction calculator with every step

The Fraction Calculator adds, subtracts, multiplies and divides fractions and mixed numbers. It shows every step of the working, free, with no account. For fraction homework, that is usually the entire assignment: common denominators, simplifying, converting mixed numbers. You can repeat examples with your own numbers and see the method, not just the final fraction.

Scientific calculator with a history tape

The Scientific Calculator handles trig, logs, powers and factorials. It keeps a history tape so you can see what you entered. That matters when you are checking a result: you can audit your own input instead of guessing where a value went wrong. The history is free and sits on the same screen.

Standard deviation calculator with full summary

The Standard Deviation Calculator accepts pasted data or a CSV upload and shows the full statistics summary. Some solver sites charge for file uploads; this one is free. You see more than a single standard deviation value. You see the working summary that lets you check the mean, sample size, and measures of spread.

Every result on these tools can be shared with a link or printed to PDF free using the buttons on each tool. That covers the “I need to hand this in” step without a paid export or account setup.

Who should use which

If your homework spans many topics, or you are working through calculus and need a solver that accepts typed problems, Mathway's breadth is genuinely hard to beat — and if you want its steps there, the subscription is the product. If your problem is narrower — fraction arithmetic you must show your working for, a statistics summary from your own data, a scientific calculation you want to audit — the free route covers it completely, with the steps included and nothing to sign up for. Plenty of students end up using both: the broad solver for the odd hard problem, and free step-showing calculators for the daily practice where the method is the lesson.
